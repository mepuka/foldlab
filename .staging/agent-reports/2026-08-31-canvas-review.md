### Front 1 — the append-only-paint claim vs the browser

| **CR-10** | SHARPEN-STATEMENT | CANVAS.md:77-81 "compression bounds every column's height by `h₀/(1−r)`, **so** the INDIVIDUATED square count on screen is bounded" | Non sequitur. Bounded height does not bound op count: 10⁷ sub-pixel squares fit inside `h₀/(1−r)` px perfectly well. What bounds the op count is the DOI THRESHOLD (or the viewport), which is a different quantity from the height bound. Arithmetic under the doc's own formula: a block is ≥1 device px while `rank ≤ ln(h₀)/ln(1/r)`. For h₀=12 px, r=0.9 → rank 24 (column 120 px tall); r=0.99 → rank 248 (1,200 px); r=0.999 → rank 2,484 (12,000 px, past any viewport). ## What survives

Ten claims took my best attack and stand unweakened. This is worth as
much as the failures.

1. **`columnBy` is a monoid homomorphism and appends localize.** Landed,
   kernel-checked, and `List.filter_append` is not going to develop a
   counterexample (`Cas/IR/Column.lean:46-49`). The incremental **data**
   story is real; only its transport into *layout* fails.
2. **`View` as a structure carrying `run_nil` + `run_append`, and
   `View.prod` closing the family** (`Cas/IR/View.lean:56-61`, `:104-115`).
   "A component reading two queries is ONE view with ONE incremental
   render" survives exactly as stated — and it is the machinery that fixes
   CR-28.
3. **Reading is total and state-free, so views compose without an
   arbiter** (SPEC.md:324-327, W1). Nothing in the pixel plane touches
   this.
4. **Rows are computable from receipts alone.** Verified field-by-field
   against `Cas/Lang/WordWire.lean:50-57`: `address`, `at`, `seq`, `size`,
   `tag` is exactly what a `Square` needs. SPEC's rows-first / bodies-on-
   demand rule (SPEC.md:461-465) stands as a structural claim; only the
   "costs nothing at ten million" gloss overreaches (CR-30).
5. **Squares as presence marks, read by position.** The Cleveland–McGill
   argument (GEOMETRY.md:47-55) is independent of everything I attacked
   and is the strongest thing in the design set. Keep squares uniform.
6. **Canvas2D is the right live handler.** Nothing I found argues for a
   different API. DOM nodes at 10⁷ are worse; SVG-DOM is worse; WebGL as a
   *later handler of the same described value* is exactly right. CV-3
   survives.
7. **Rolling our own hit-testing is correct** — and better justified than
   the doc knows, since the platform's `addHitRegion` was removed from the
   spec in 2016 (CR-33).
8. **`hit` really is a partial inverse on the individuated window.** Once
   the type gains its third arm and its ≥1-device-pixel side condition
   (CR-19, CR-20), the claim is provable and cheap.
9. **Two handlers over one described value** — the register/handler
   doctrine at the pixel plane. What failed is the *strength* of the
   agreement (raster → geometric, CR-23), not the shape. CV-1 and CV-2
   survive as restructured, and CR-24's shared `place` function makes CV-2
   stronger than proposed, not weaker.
10. **Append-only paint survives as an epoch-local fast path.** Within a
    fixed viewport, DPR, scroll offset and DOI partition, extending the
    word really does mean drawing only the new ops with no clear and no
    diff. That is a true, provable, useful claim. It is simply not the
    architecture; it is an optimisation inside one.

And two independent confirmations worth banking: foldkit's shipped
`toCanvasPoint` already solves the CSS-pixel-vs-backing-store and scroll
terms of front 3 correctly, and foldkit's hydration does fire `insert`
hooks on adopted nodes, so an SSR'd canvas does get painted at hydrate.
Two worries on the brief's list turn out to be already handled.

## The five to fix before any code is written, ranked

1. **CR-1 / CR-3 — decide whether layout is monotone under append.**
   Recency-compressed heights and focus-driven DOI both make layout a
   function of the whole word, which falsifies the extension law
   (CANVAS.md:51-55) that the entire pipeline is derived from. The estate
   already has the ruling that settles it — QUERIES.md:98-100, patch iff
   monotone, otherwise compute at a cut — and it was never applied to
   layout. Nothing else on this list can be specified until this is ruled,
   because it decides what the Scene *is*.

2. **CR-5 / CR-6 / CR-9 — the canvas is a viewport, not the document, and
   the repaint-trigger set must be written down.** Chromium's 65,535-pixel
   dimension cap and 268-megapixel area cap mean the one-eternal-canvas
   design fails around 4×10³–10⁴ receipts, silently and with no exception,
   against a design target of 10⁷ — and setting `width`/`height` clears the
   bitmap by spec, on the exact event the design calls cheap. Virtualize,
   then restate "positions never move" in document space and enumerate the
   epoch terminators.

3. **CR-28 / CR-29 — name the column fold's carrier, and pay its price.**
   `View.prod (View.height t) (View.lastK t k)` — a count plus the last `k`
   receipts — is what makes the placement computable at 10⁷ in about a
   megabyte, versus ~1.5–2 GB for the landed `View.column` carrier. `lastK`
   is a genuine `View` instance and the proof is four lines. The price is
   real and must be stated: with a bounded window, a strip cannot be
   individuated without a **ranged** read, and the ratified reader has only
   `since(n)` with no limit. Either serve QUERIES.md's Q-SEG or ship v1
   with pure-recency DOI and unclickable strips.

4. **CR-12 / CR-19 / CR-25 — fix the carrier's types and the snapping
   rule, together.** The op union mixes index space and pixel space and no
   op carries a rectangle, so the Scene does not determine pixels at all;
   `hit`'s type is wrong once strips exist; and "crisp integer-snapped
   pixels at DPR" is asserted without a rule. All three are the same
   omission: the function `place : Placement × Viewport × DPR → Rect[]` is
   missing. Write it — fill only, never stroke, integers in device space —
   and CR-14's pixel-disjointness, CR-24's agreement gate and CR-19's
   inverse all become statable in one move.

5. **CR-16 / CR-23 / CR-31 — drop `Label` from the op union, weaken the
   agreement claim to geometric, and make the SVG register the canvas's
   fallback content.** These are one decision. A rects-only union is what
   makes handler agreement achievable at all (no font metrics); the spec
   guarantees no pixel identity between any two rasterizers, so the gate
   must compare coordinates; and the WHATWG spec *requires* fallback
   content that conveys the canvas's purpose, which the SVG string already
   is. One artifact then serves the golden-byte gate, SSR, export, and
   accessibility — and it is what makes the trunk addressable by foldkit's
   own accessible-locator Scene tests at all.
