# Detailed Solution to the Cell Loop Partition Problem

## Step 1: Verify the Cell Loop Formula

For an $a \times b$ cell loop:
- Top row: $a$ cells
- Bottom row: $a$ cells  
- Left column: $b$ cells
- Right column: $b$ cells
- Subtract 4 corner cells counted twice: $a + a + b + b - 4 = 2a + 2b - 4$ ✓

## Step 2: Set Up the Constraint Equation

We need 5 loops with total cells = 100:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{5} (2a_i + 2b_i - 4) = 100$$

Simplifying:
$$2\sum_{i=1}^{5}(a_i + b_i) - 20 = 100$$
$$\sum_{i=1}^{5}(a_i + b_i) = 60$$

Each pair must satisfy $2 \leq a_i, b_i \leq 10$.

## Step 3: Enumerate Valid Dimension Sets

The constraint is $\sum_{i=1}^{5}(a_i + b_i) = 60$, with each dimension in $[2,10]$.

## Step 4: Determine Geometric Feasibility

**Critical insight:** The loops must partition the entire $10 \times 10$ grid (no overlaps, no gaps). This means:
- Total cells occupied = 100 ✓
- Complete coverage required

For a valid partition, I'll test nested concentric rectangular frames with unit thickness.

**Testing nested concentric squares:**

For concentrically nested loops in a $10 \times 10$ grid with unit-width frames between levels:

| Loop | Dimensions | Cells | Sum |
|------|-----------|-------|-----|
| Outer frame | $10 \times 10$ | $2(10) + 2(10) - 4 = 36$ | 20 |
| 2nd frame | $8 \times 8$ | $2(8) + 2(8) - 4 = 28$ | 16 |
| 3rd frame | $6 \times 6$ | $2(6) + 2(6) - 4 = 20$ | 12 |
| 4th frame | $4 \times 4$ | $2(4) + 2(4) - 4 = 12$ | 8 |
| Inner frame | $2 \times 2$ | $2(2) + 2(2) - 4 = 4$ | 4 |

**Verification:**
- Total cells: $36 + 28 + 20 + 12 + 4 = 100$ ✓
- Sum constraint: $20 + 16 + 12 + 8 + 4 = 60$ ✓
- All dimensions in $[2,10]$ ✓
- Perfect nesting: each inner rectangle is exactly 2 units smaller in each dimension ✓

**Why is this the only solution?**

For nested rectangular loops with unit-width frames:
- If outer dimensions are $a_1 \times b_1$, the inner must be $(a_1-2) \times (b_1-2)$
- Starting with $10 \times 10$ grid, we must have $a_1 = 10, b_1 = 10$
- This forces: $10 \times 10 \to 8 \times 8 \to 6 \times 6 \to 4 \times 4 \to 2 \times 2$
- No other dimension sequences can fit 5 loops perfectly in a $10 \times 10$ grid without gaps or overlaps

Non-concentric tilings (e.g., side-by-side frames or other configurations) cannot satisfy the requirement that exactly 100 cells are partitioned with these geometric constraints.

## Step 5: Count Distinct Placements

For the unique dimension set $\{(10,10), (8,8), (6,6), (4,4), (2,2)\}$:

The positioning is uniquely determined by geometric constraints:
- The $10 \times 10$ loop occupies the boundary of the grid
- The $8 \times 8$ loop must be centered with exactly 1-cell margin: rows/cols 1-8 (0-indexed)
- The $6 \times 6$ loop occupies rows/cols 2-7
- The $4 \times 4$ loop occupies rows/cols 3-6
- The $2 \times 2$ loop occupies rows/cols 4-5

There is **exactly one spatial configuration** (the centered nested arrangement).
