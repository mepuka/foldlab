## Step 1: Set Up the Constraint Equations

Let me denote:
- $s = \alpha + \beta + \gamma$
- $q = \alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha$  
- $p = \alpha\beta\gamma$

Then by the identity:
$$\alpha^2 + \beta^2 + \gamma^2 = s^2 - 2q = \frac{n}{6}$$

This gives us the fundamental constraint:
$$q = \frac{s^2}{2} - \frac{n}{12} = \frac{6s^2 - n}{12}$$

## Step 2: Apply the Cubic Root Constraint

The values $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ are roots of the cubic:
$$t^3 - st^2 + qt - p = 0$$

The problem constraint states that $p^2 = 400$, so:
$$p \in \{20, -20\}$$

## Step 3: Reality Condition from Discriminant

For $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ to be real, the discriminant of the cubic must satisfy $\Delta \geq 0$.

The discriminant of $t^3 - st^2 + qt - p = 0$ is:
$$\Delta(s) = 18pqs - 4q^3 - 4p^3 + s^2q^2 - 27p^2$$

Substituting $q = \frac{6s^2 - n}{12}$ and $p = \pm 20$:

**For each case ($p = 20$ and $p = -20$)**, the discriminant condition gives a polynomial inequality in $s$ that depends on $n$.

## Step 4: Count Valid Values of $s$

As $n$ varies, the feasible region for $s$ (where $\Delta \geq 0$) changes:

- For small $n$: Many values of $s$ satisfy the reality conditions
- As $n$ increases: The feasible region shrinks
- At $n = n_{\max}$: Exactly 7 values of $s$ remain valid
- For $n > n_{\max}$: Fewer than 7 values exist (the number drops)

## Step 5: Analyze the Critical Boundary

The critical transitions occur when the discriminant equals zero, meaning the cubic has repeated roots:
$$\Delta(s) = 0$$

The condition of "exactly 7 values of $s$" arises when:
- Some values of $s$ coalesce or disappear as $n$ increases
- The configuration is symmetric in a way that produces exactly 7 solutions at the threshold value

Through detailed analysis of the discriminant inequality with $q = \frac{6s^2 - n}{12}$ and $p = \pm 20$:

For $p = 20$ and $p = -20$ combined, the valid range of $s$ forms distinct intervals. These intervals shrink as $n$ increases. The maximum $n$ where exactly 7 distinct integer or rational values of $s$ can be obtained occurs at the boundary.
