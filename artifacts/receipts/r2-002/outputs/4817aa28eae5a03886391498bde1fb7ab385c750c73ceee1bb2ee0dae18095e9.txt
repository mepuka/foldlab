# Detailed Solution

## Step 1: Set up coordinate system
- Place $B$ at the origin: $B = (0, 0)$
- Place $A$ on the positive $x$-axis: $A = (1, 0)$

## Step 2: Find $A'$ after first rotation
Rotating $A$ counterclockwise by angle $\theta$ around $B = (0,0)$:
$$A' = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$$

## Step 3: Verify distance $A'B$
$$|A'B| = \sqrt{\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta} = \sqrt{1} = 1 \,\checkmark$$

The rotation preserves the unit distance.

## Step 4: Find $B'$ after second rotation
We rotate $B = (0,0)$ clockwise by angle $\theta$ around $A' = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.

For a clockwise rotation by $\theta$ around point $P$, the formula is:
$$B' = P + R_{-\theta}(B - P)$$

where $R_{-\theta}$ is the rotation matrix for angle $-\theta$:
$$R_{-\theta} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

Computing:
$$B - A' = (0,0) - (\cos\theta, \sin\theta) = (-\cos\theta, -\sin\theta)$$

$$R_{-\theta}(-\cos\theta, -\sin\theta)^T = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -\cos\theta \\ -\sin\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

$$= \begin{pmatrix} -\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta \\ \sin\theta\cos\theta - \sin\theta\cos\theta \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

Therefore:
$$B' = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta) + (-1, 0) = (\cos\theta - 1, \sin\theta)$$

## Step 5: Apply the distance constraint $AB' = \frac{4}{3}$

Calculate $|AB'|^2$ where $A = (1, 0)$ and $B' = (\cos\theta - 1, \sin\theta)$:

$$|AB'|^2 = ((\cos\theta - 1) - 1)^2 + (\sin\theta - 0)^2$$

$$= (\cos\theta - 2)^2 + \sin^2\theta$$

$$= \cos^2\theta - 4\cos\theta + 4 + \sin^2\theta$$

$$= (\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) - 4\cos\theta + 4$$

$$= 1 - 4\cos\theta + 4 = 5 - 4\cos\theta$$

Setting $|AB'|^2 = \left(\frac{4}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{16}{9}$:

$$5 - 4\cos\theta = \frac{16}{9}$$

## Step 6: Solve for $\cos\theta$

$$-4\cos\theta = \frac{16}{9} - 5$$

$$-4\cos\theta = \frac{16}{9} - \frac{45}{9} = \frac{-29}{9}$$

$$\cos\theta = \frac{29}{36}$$

**Verification:** $\frac{29}{36}$ is in lowest terms since $29$ is prime and doesn't divide $36$, so $\gcd(29, 36) = 1$.

## Step 7: Express answer in required form

$$\cos\theta = \frac{29}{36} = \frac{m}{n}$$

where $m = 29$ and $n = 36$ with $\gcd(m,n) = 1$.

$$m + n = 29 + 36 = 65$$

Final answer (single integer only): **65**
