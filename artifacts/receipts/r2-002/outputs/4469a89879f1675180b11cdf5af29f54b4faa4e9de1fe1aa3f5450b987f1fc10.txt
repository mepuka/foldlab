# Detailed Solution

## Step 1: Express distances from vertices to incenter

For any triangle, the distance from vertex to incenter is given by:
$$AI = \frac{r}{\sin(A/2)}$$

Since the triangle is isosceles with $AB = BC = c$, we have $\angle A = \angle C$, therefore:
$$AI = IC = \frac{r}{\sin(A/2)}$$

## Step 2: Set up the perimeter ratio equation

- Perimeter of $\triangle ABC$: $P_1 = AB + BC + AC = c + c + b = 2c + b$
- Perimeter of $\triangle AIC$: $P_2 = AI + IC + AC = 2 \cdot AI + b$

Given the ratio:
$$\frac{2c + b}{2 \cdot AI + b} = \frac{125}{6}$$

## Step 3: Solve for $AI$

Cross-multiplying:
$$6(2c + b) = 125(2 \cdot AI + b)$$
$$12c + 6b = 250 \cdot AI + 125b$$
$$12c - 119b = 250 \cdot AI$$
$$AI = \frac{12c - 119b}{250}$$

## Step 4: Apply triangle inequality constraints

**For $\triangle ABC$:** $2c > b$ (isosceles condition)

**For $\triangle AIC$:** $2 \cdot AI > b$
$$2 \cdot \frac{12c - 119b}{250} > b$$
$$24c - 238b > 250b$$
$$24c > 488b$$
$$c > \frac{61b}{3}$$

## Step 5: Find integer solutions

For both $c$ and $b$ to be integers with $AI$ being an integer, let $AI = n$ where $n$ is a positive integer:
$$12c - 119b = 250n$$
$$c = \frac{119b + 250n}{12}$$

For $c$ to be an integer, we need $119b + 250n \equiv 0 \pmod{12}$.

Since $119 \equiv 11 \pmod{12}$ and $250 \equiv 10 \pmod{12}$:
$$11b + 10n \equiv 0 \pmod{12}$$

Testing with $b = 12k$ and $n = 6m$:
- When $m = 2$: $n = 12$
- Then $c = \frac{119(12k) + 250(12)}{12} = 119k + 250$

The constraint $c > \frac{61b}{3} = \frac{61(12k)}{3} = 244k$ gives:
$$119k + 250 > 244k$$
$$250 > 125k$$
$$k < 2$$

## Step 6: Test the minimum case

With $k = 1$: $b = 12$, $c = 369$, $AI = 12$

**Verification:**
- Triangle inequality: $2c = 738 > 12 = b$ ✓
- Triangle AIC inequality: $2 \cdot AI = 24 > 12 = b$ ✓
- Perimeter ratio: 
$$\frac{2(369) + 12}{2(12) + 12} = \frac{750}{36} = \frac{125}{6}$$ ✓

All conditions are satisfied!

**Final Answer: 369**
