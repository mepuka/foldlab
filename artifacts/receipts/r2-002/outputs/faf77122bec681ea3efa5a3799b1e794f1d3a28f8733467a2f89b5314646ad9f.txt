# Solution: Sum of Radii for Tangent Circles

## Step 1: Rewrite the parabola in standard form

Given: $2y = x^2 - 8x + 12$

Dividing by 2:
$$y = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 4x + 6$$

Completing the square:
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x + 16 - 16) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 8 + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$$

**Vertex:** $(4, -2)$ | **Opens upward**

## Step 2: Find the tangent line slope

For the parabola $y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$:

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = (x-4)$$

At point $(a, b)$ on the parabola: **slope = $a - 4$**

## Step 3: Apply the tangency requirement

For a circle centered at $(4, 39)$ to be tangent to the parabola at $(a, b)$, the radius must be perpendicular to the tangent line.

- Slope of radius: $\frac{b-39}{a-4}$
- Slope of tangent: $a-4$
- Perpendicularity condition: $(a-4) \cdot \frac{b-39}{a-4} = -1$

Simplifying:
$$b - 39 = -1$$
$$b = 38$$

## Step 4: Find the tangency point(s)

Substitute $b = 38$ into the parabola equation:
$$38 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2 - 2$$
$$40 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2$$
$$(a-4)^2 = 80$$
$$a = 4 \pm \sqrt{80} = 4 \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$

Two tangency points: $(4 + 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$ and $(4 - 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$

## Step 5: Calculate the radius for each tangency point

For each tangency point $(a, 38)$:
$$r = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + (38-39)^2}$$
$$r = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + 1}$$
$$r = \sqrt{80 + 1}$$
$$r = \sqrt{81}$$
$$r = 9$$

Both tangency points yield **$r = 9$**

## Step 6: Find the sum

$$r_1 + r_2 = 9 + 9 = 18$$

---

**18**
