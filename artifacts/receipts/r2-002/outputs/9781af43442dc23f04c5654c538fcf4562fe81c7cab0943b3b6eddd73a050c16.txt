# Solution Plan for Finding Minimum Divisors of Imbalanced $M$

## 1. **Establish the Imbalance Threshold**
   - For $M$ to be imbalanced, need $d(n) > 0.99 \cdot \tau(M)$ where $d(n)$ counts divisors $< 0.01M$
   - This means at least $\lceil 0.99\tau(M) + 1 \rceil$ divisors must be small
   - Equivalently: at most $\lfloor 0.01\tau(M) \rfloor$ divisors can be $\geq 0.01M$ (large divisors)

## 2. **Count Large Divisors for $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b \cdot \prod p_i^{c_i}$**
   - A divisor $2^{a'} \cdot 5^{b'} \cdot \prod p_i^{c'_i}$ is large iff $2^{a'} \cdot 5^{b'} \cdot \prod p_i^{c'_i} \geq 0.01M$
   - For "simple" forms (e.g., $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b$), count large divisors systematically
   - Use the constraint: number of large divisors $\leq \lfloor 0.01\tau(M) \rfloor$

## 3. **Test Small Cases with Minimal Prime Factorization**
   - Start with $M = 2^4 \cdot 5^3$ (minimum required by divisibility)
   - Then test forms: $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b$ for increasing exponents
   - Then consider adding small primes (3, 7, 11, etc.)
   - For each candidate, calculate $\tau(M)$ and verify the imbalance condition

## 4. **Verify the Imbalance Condition**
   - For each candidate $M$:
     - Compute $\tau(M)$
     - Count divisors $d < 0.01M$
     - Check if count $> 0.99 \cdot \tau(M)$
   - Identify the smallest $\tau(M)$ that satisfies this condition

## 5. **Compare Candidates Across Different Structures**
   - Compare pure form: $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b$ 
   - Compare with one additional prime: $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b \cdot p$
   - Compare with two additional primes: $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b \cdot p \cdot q$
   - Identify which structure minimizes $\tau(M)$ while maintaining imbalance

## 6. **Confirm the Answer**
   - Report the minimum value of $\tau(M)$ found
   - Provide the corresponding form of $M$ as verification
