## Step 1-2: Set Up the Roots of Unity Filter

$$S_r = \frac{1}{502}\left[2^{10000} + \sum_{j=1}^{501} \omega^{-jr} (1+\omega^j)^{10000}\right]$$

where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/502}$ is a primitive 502-nd root of unity.

## Step 3: Establish the Divisibility Condition

For $503 \mid S_r$, we need:
$$2^{10000} + \sum_{j=1}^{501} \omega^{-jr} (1+\omega^j)^{10000} \equiv 0 \pmod{503}$$

(Since $502 \equiv -1 \pmod{503}$, we have $502^{-1} \equiv -1 \pmod{503}$)

## Step 4: Apply Fermat's Little Theorem

Since 503 is prime: $2^{502} \equiv 1 \pmod{503}$

Computing $10000 \bmod 502$:
$$10000 = 19 \times 502 + 462$$

Therefore: $2^{10000} \equiv 2^{462} \pmod{503}$

## Step 5: Compute $2^{462} \pmod{503}$

$$2^{462} = 2^{502-40} = \frac{2^{502}}{2^{40}} \equiv 2^{-40} \pmod{503}$$

**Computing $2^{40} \bmod 503$ by repeated squaring:**
- $2^8 = 256$
- $2^{16} \equiv 256^2 = 65536 \equiv 146 \pmod{503}$
- $2^{32} \equiv 146^2 = 21316 \equiv 190 \pmod{503}$
- $2^{40} = 2^{32} \cdot 2^8 \equiv 190 \cdot 256 = 48640 \equiv 352 \pmod{503}$

**Finding the modular inverse of 352:**

Using the extended Euclidean algorithm:
- $503 = 1 \times 352 + 151$
- $352 = 2 \times 151 + 50$
- $151 = 3 \times 50 + 1$

Back-substituting: $1 = 7 \times 503 - 10 \times 352$

Therefore: $352^{-1} \equiv -10 \equiv 493 \pmod{503}$

So $2^{462} \equiv 493 \pmod{503}$

$$\sum_{j=1}^{501} \omega^{-jr} (1+\omega^j)^{10000} \equiv -493 \pmod{503}$$
