# Extended Solution for Hamiltonian Paths in 3×10 Grid

## Step 1: Understand the Constraint Structure

- Grid: 3 rows × 10 columns = 30 cells total
- Start point A: (2,2) - middle row, column 2
- End point B: (2,9) - middle row, column 9
- Both endpoints in the same (middle) row creates severe constraints

## Step 2: Analyze Forced Structure

The 3-row height means:
- Vertical movement between rows is expensive (limited capacity)
- Both A and B are in row 2, separated by 7 cells: (2,3) through (2,8)
- To the left of A: 1 cell (2,1)
- To the right of B: 1 cell (2,10)

## Step 4: Forced Path Segments Analysis

Starting from A=(2,2), the path must navigate:
- Leftmost region: column 1 (cells 1,1 / 2,1 / 3,1)
- Middle region: columns 3-8 in row 2
- Rightmost region: column 10 and ending at column 9

The constraint that path starts at column 2 and ends at column 9 (both in row 2) creates a critical bottleneck: cells (2,1) and (2,10) must be visited via specific routing patterns.

## Step 5: Systematic Case Analysis

The path must handle:
1. Visit (2,1): Left from A or return via rows 1/3

For a valid path:

- (2,10) must be visited (forced by connectivity)

**Analysis shows:**
- Path must make 2-4 excursions between row 2 and rows 1/3
- Each excursion must respect visited cell constraints
- The middle columns (3-10) force specific snake patterns
