# Detailed Solution

## Step 1: Set Up the Problem

On each die roll:
- Rolls 1-2: Alice gets a coin (probability 1/3)
- Rolls 3-4: Bob gets a coin (probability 1/3)  
- Rolls 5-6: Carol gets a coin (probability 1/3)

We want: **P(Alice ≥ 2 AND Bob ≥ 2 before Carol gets any coin)**

## Step 2: Condition on When Carol First Rolls a Coin

Let $n$ = total number of rolls before Carol's first coin appears.

- P(Carol's first coin on roll $n+1$) = $(2/3)^n \cdot (1/3)$
- The first $n$ rolls are distributed between Alice and Bob only
- Each of these $n$ rolls goes to Alice or Bob with equal probability 1/2

## Step 3: Set Up the Total Probability

For exactly $n$ rolls before Carol gets a coin:

$$P(\text{target}) = \sum_{n=4}^{\infty} P(\text{$n$ rolls before Carol}) \cdot P(\text{both } \geq 2 \mid n \text{ rolls})$$

For the conditional probability with $n$ rolls distributed equally between A and B:
$$P(\text{A} \geq 2 \text{ and B} \geq 2 \mid n) = \sum_{a=2}^{n-2} \binom{n}{a} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n$$

## Step 4: Simplify the Combinatorial Sum

We need: $\sum_{a=2}^{n-2} \binom{n}{a}$

Since $\sum_{a=0}^{n} \binom{n}{a} = 2^n$:

$$\sum_{a=2}^{n-2} \binom{n}{a} = 2^n - \binom{n}{0} - \binom{n}{1} - \binom{n}{n-1} - \binom{n}{n}$$
$$= 2^n - 1 - n - n - 1 = 2^n - 2n - 2$$

## Step 5: Calculate the Main Probability

$$P(\text{target}) = \sum_{n=4}^{\infty} (2/3)^n \cdot (1/3) \cdot [2^n - 2n - 2] \cdot (1/2)^n$$

$$= \frac{1}{3} \sum_{n=4}^{\infty} (1/3)^n \cdot [2^n - 2n - 2]$$

$$= \frac{1}{3} \left[\sum_{n=4}^{\infty} (2/3)^n - 2\sum_{n=4}^{\infty} n(1/3)^n - 2\sum_{n=4}^{\infty} (1/3)^n\right]$$

## Step 6: Evaluate Each Geometric/Arithmetic Series

**Sum 1:** $\sum_{n=4}^{\infty} (2/3)^n = \frac{(2/3)^4}{1-2/3} = \frac{16/81}{1/3} = \frac{16}{27}$

**Sum 2:** For $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n \cdot x^n = \frac{x}{(1-x)^2}$, when $x = 1/3$:
- $\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} n(1/3)^n = \frac{1/3}{(2/3)^2} = \frac{3}{4}$
- $\sum_{n=0}^{3} n(1/3)^n = 0 + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{2}{9} + \frac{3}{27} = \frac{18}{27} = \frac{2}{3}$
- $\sum_{n=4}^{\infty} n(1/3)^n = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1}{12}$

**Sum 3:** $\sum_{n=4}^{\infty} (1/3)^n = \frac{(1/3)^4}{1-1/3} = \frac{1/81}{2/3} = \frac{1}{54}$

## Step 7: Compute the Final Result

$$P(\text{target}) = \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{16}{27} - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{12} - 2 \cdot \frac{1}{54}\right]$$

$$= \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{16}{27} - \frac{1}{6} - \frac{1}{27}\right]$$

$$= \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{15}{27} - \frac{1}{6}\right] = \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{5}{9} - \frac{1}{6}\right]$$

$$= \frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{10}{18} - \frac{3}{18}\right] = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{7}{18} = \frac{7}{54}$$

## Step 8: Final Answer

The probability in lowest terms is $\frac{7}{54}$ where $\gcd(7, 54) = 1$.

Therefore: $m = 7$, $n = 54$
