# Competition Math Problem Analysis

## Given Quantities and Conditions

**Structure:**
- A right prism with two congruent regular decagon bases
- Bottom base: vertices $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{10}$
- Top base: vertices $B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_{10}$
- Total of 20 vertices in set $S$

**Constraints on the Plane:**
- The plane does NOT pass through any vertex of $S$
- The plane partitions the 20 vertices into exactly two non-empty sets
- One of these two sets is called $M$

**Key Constraint on $M$:**
- The vertices in $M$ must be on one side of the plane
- The vertices not in $M$ must be on the other side of the plane
- This means $M$ must form a **convex polytope** (the intersection of the prism with a half-space)

## What is Being Asked

**Find:** The number of distinct possible sets $M$ that can arise from all such valid plane partitions.

In other words: How many different subsets of the 20 vertices can be separated to one side of a plane that doesn't pass through any vertex?

**Note:** Since swapping the two sides of the plane gives sets $M$ and its complement, and the problem treats these as distinct (asking for "possible sets $M$"), we count both a set and its complement as different answers.
