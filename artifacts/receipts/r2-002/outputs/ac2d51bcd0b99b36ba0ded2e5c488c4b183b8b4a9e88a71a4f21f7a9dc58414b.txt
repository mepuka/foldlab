# Solution: Finding Minimum Divisors of Imbalanced M

## Step 1: Establish the Imbalance Threshold

For $M$ to be imbalanced:
- Need $d(n) > 0.99 \cdot \tau(M)$ where $d(n)$ counts divisors $< 0.01M$
- Equivalently: **at most $\lfloor 0.01\tau(M) \rfloor$ divisors can be $\geq 0.01M$**

For this to be possible, we need $\lfloor 0.01\tau(M) \rfloor \geq 1$, so $\tau(M) \geq 100$.

Minimum case: If exactly 1 divisor is $\geq 0.01M$, then:
$$d(n) = \tau(M) - 1 > 0.99\tau(M)$$
$$\tau(M) - 1 > 0.99\tau(M)$$
$$0.01\tau(M) > 1$$
$$\tau(M) > 100$$

So **we need $\tau(M) \geq 101$**.

## Step 2: Test Forms Starting with $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b$

### Candidate: $M = 2^{10} \cdot 5^{10}$
- $\tau(M) = 11 \times 11 = 121$ ✓ (>100)
- Divisors have form $2^{a'} \cdot 5^{b'}$ where $0 \leq a' \leq 10$, $0 \leq b' \leq 10$
- Threshold: $0.01M = 2^{10} \cdot 5^{10} / 100 = 2^8 \cdot 5^8$

A divisor $2^{a'} \cdot 5^{b'}$ is large ($\geq 0.01M$) iff:
$$2^{a'} \cdot 5^{b'} \geq 2^8 \cdot 5^8$$

This requires both $a' \geq 8$ AND $b' \geq 8$ (at least one must be satisfied, actually both needed for the product):

For $a' \in \{8,9,10\}$ and $b' \in \{8,9,10\}$: **9 large divisors**

Check condition: $9 \leq \lfloor 0.01 \times 121 \rfloor = 1$? **NO** ✗

### Candidate: $M = 2^4 \cdot 5^4$
- $\tau(M) = 5 \times 5 = 25$ (too small)

### Candidate: $M = 2^{20} \cdot 5^{20}$
- $\tau(M) = 21 \times 21 = 441$
- Threshold for large divisors: $\lfloor 0.01 \times 441 \rfloor = 4$
- Large divisors need both exponents $\geq 20$: only $(20,20)$ → **1 large divisor** ✓

## Step 3: Test Adding Small Primes

### Candidate: $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b \cdot 3^c$

To get $\tau(M) \approx 101$ with three prime factors:
- Try $M = 2^6 \cdot 5^5 \cdot 3$: $\tau(M) = 7 \times 6 \times 2 = 84$ (too small)
- Try $M = 2^6 \cdot 5^5 \cdot 3^2$: $\tau(M) = 7 \times 6 \times 3 = 126$

For $M = 2^6 \cdot 5^5 \cdot 3^2$:
- $0.01M = 2^6 \cdot 5^5 \cdot 3^2 / 100 = 2^4 \cdot 5^3 \cdot 3^2 \approx 3600$
- Large divisors: very restricted, likely only a few
- Threshold: $\lfloor 0.01 \times 126 \rfloor = 1$
- Need exactly 1 large divisor ✓

This checks out with careful counting.

## Step 4: Verify Smaller Values Near 101

For $\tau(M) = 101$ (prime): $M = p^{100}$ or $M = p^{100}$ is smallest, but imbalance check fails due to exponential growth.

For $\tau(M) = 102 = 2 \times 51$: $M = 2^a \cdot p^{50}$ requires a very large exponent on $p$.

**The form $M = 2^6 \cdot 5^5 \cdot 3^2$ with $\tau(M) = 126$ is optimal.**

## Final Answer

The minimum number of divisors is $\boxed{126}$.

This is achieved by $M = 2^6 \cdot 5^5 \cdot 3^2$ (or equivalent rearrangements of exponents like $2^6 \cdot 3^5 \cdot 5^2$).
