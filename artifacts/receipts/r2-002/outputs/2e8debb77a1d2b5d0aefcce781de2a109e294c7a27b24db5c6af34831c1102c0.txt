# Analysis of the Problem

## Given Quantities and Conditions

1. **The set S**: All positive integers less than 143 that are relatively prime to 143
   - That is, $S = \{n : 1 \leq n < 143, \gcd(n, 143) = 1\}$

2. **The number 143**: 
   - Note that $143 = 11 \times 13$ (product of two primes)
   - So $\gcd(n, 143) = 1$ iff $n$ is not divisible by 11 or 13

3. **Cardinality of S**:
   - By Euler's totient function: $\phi(143) = \phi(11 \times 13) = (11-1)(13-1) = 10 \times 12 = 120$
   - Therefore $|S| = 120$

4. **Constraint on triples**: 
   - $(a,b,c)$ must be an ordered triple
   - All three elements must be from $S$
   - The equation $a + b = c$ must hold

## What is Being Asked

Count the number of ordered triples $(a,b,c)$ where:
- $a \in S$, $b \in S$, $c \in S$
- $a + b = c$

Equivalently: for how many pairs $(a,b)$ with $a, b \in S$ is their sum $c = a+b$ also in $S$?
