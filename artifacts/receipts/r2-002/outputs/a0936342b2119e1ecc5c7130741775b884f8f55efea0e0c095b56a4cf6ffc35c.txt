# Solution: Finding $\cos\theta$

## Step 1: Coordinate System Setup
- Place $B$ at the origin: $B = (0, 0)$
- Place $A$ on the positive x-axis: $A = (1, 0)$

## Step 2: Find Coordinates of $A'$

Rotating $A = (1, 0)$ counterclockwise by angle $\theta$ around $B = (0, 0)$ using the rotation matrix:

$$A' = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta \\ \sin\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

## Step 3: Verify Distance $A'B$

$$|A'B| = \sqrt{\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta} = 1 \quad \checkmark$$

The rotation correctly preserves the unit distance.

## Step 4: Find Coordinates of $B'$

Rotate $B = (0, 0)$ clockwise by angle $\theta$ around center $A' = (\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$.

**Translate** $B$ to make $A'$ the origin:
$$B - A' = (0, 0) - (\cos\theta, \sin\theta) = (-\cos\theta, -\sin\theta)$$

**Apply clockwise rotation** (rotation by $-\theta$):
$$\begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} -\cos\theta \\ -\sin\theta \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta \\ \sin\theta\cos\theta - \sin\theta\cos\theta \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

**Translate back** by adding $A'$:
$$B' = \begin{pmatrix} -1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta \\ \sin\theta \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta - 1 \\ \sin\theta \end{pmatrix}$$

## Step 5: Apply the Distance Constraint

Given $|AB'| = \frac{4}{3}$, where $A = (1, 0)$ and $B' = (\cos\theta - 1, \sin\theta)$:

$$|AB'|^2 = (\cos\theta - 1 - 1)^2 + (\sin\theta - 0)^2 = \frac{16}{9}$$

$$(\cos\theta - 2)^2 + \sin^2\theta = \frac{16}{9}$$

Expand the left side:
$$\cos^2\theta - 4\cos\theta + 4 + \sin^2\theta = \frac{16}{9}$$

Using the Pythagorean identity $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$:
$$1 - 4\cos\theta + 4 = \frac{16}{9}$$

$$5 - 4\cos\theta = \frac{16}{9}$$

## Step 6: Solve for $\cos\theta$

$$-4\cos\theta = \frac{16}{9} - 5 = \frac{16 - 45}{9} = \frac{-29}{9}$$

$$\cos\theta = \frac{29}{36}$$

## Step 7: Express in Required Form

With $\cos\theta = \frac{29}{36}$, we have $m = 29$ and $n = 36$.

Verify $\gcd(29, 36) = 1$: Since 29 is prime and doesn't divide 36, they are coprime. ✓

$$m + n = 29 + 36 = 65$$

**65**
