# Solution Plan for Equiangular Pentagon Problem

1. **Set up the expansion of the perimeter squared**
   - Expand $P^2 = (s_1 + s_2 + s_3 + s_4 + s_5)^2$
   - This gives: $P^2 = \sum s_i^2 + 2\sum_{i<j} s_i s_j$
   - Substitute known value: $P^2 = 308 + 2\sum_{i<j} s_i s_j$

2. **Use the constraint from diagonal lengths**
   - Apply the law of cosines to each diagonal in the equiangular pentagon
   - Since each interior angle is 108°, relate diagonals to sides using this angle
   - Express $\sum d_k^2$ in terms of the sides and their products
   - Use the given condition: $\sum d_k^2 = 800$

3. **Establish the relationship between sides and diagonal sum of squares**
   - For an equiangular pentagon with all angles 108°, derive that each diagonal connects vertices separated by specific geometric relationships
   - Create an equation relating $\sum d_k^2$ to $\sum s_i^2$ and $\sum_{i<j} s_i s_j$
   - (Key insight: The cosine of 108° = $\cos(108°) = -\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{4}$ may appear)

4. **Solve for the cross-product term**
   - From the diagonal constraint equation, isolate $\sum_{i<j} s_i s_j$
   - This will likely involve $\sqrt{5}$ terms given the geometry of regular/equiangular pentagons

5. **Calculate P²**
   - Substitute the value of $\sum_{i<j} s_i s_j$ back into: $P^2 = 308 + 2\sum_{i<j} s_i s_j$
   - Simplify to obtain $P^2$ in the form $m\sqrt{n}$

6. **Identify m and n, then report the answer**
   - Verify that $n$ is square-free
   - Calculate and report $m + n$
