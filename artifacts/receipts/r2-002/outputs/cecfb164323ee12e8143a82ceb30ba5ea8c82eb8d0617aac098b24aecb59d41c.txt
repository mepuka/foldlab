# Systematic Enumeration of Half-Space Intersections for a Decagonal Prism

## Step 1: Identify the Structure of the Decagonal Prism

The decagonal prism has:
- **Vertices:** 20 (10 on bottom base B, 10 on top base T)
- **Edges:** 30 (10 on B + 10 on T + 10 vertical)
- **Faces:** 12 (2 decagonal + 10 rectangular)

## Step 2: Classify by Dimension

For a set M of vertices to be realizable as M = {vertices of P ∩ H⁺}, we need to count all convex polytopes that can arise from intersecting the prism with half-spaces.

### **Type A: 0-dimensional (Single Vertices)**
Any single vertex can be isolated by a hyperplane.
- Count: **20**

### **Type B: 1-dimensional (Edges)**
All edges of the prism can be isolated:
- Bottom base edges: 10
- Top base edges: 10  
- Vertical edges: 10
- Count: **30**

### **Type C: 2-dimensional Faces**
All faces of the prism can be isolated:
- Decagonal bases: 2
- Rectangular lateral faces: 10
- Count: **12**

### **Type D: Partial Faces (Cutting through faces)**

When a hyperplane cuts through a face but doesn't isolate the entire face, we get partial face regions.

**For each decagonal base (2 bases):**
- Subsets of 3 vertices: C(10,3) = 120 per base
- Subsets of 4 vertices: C(10,4) = 210 per base
- Subsets of 5 vertices: C(10,5) = 252 per base
- Subsets of 6 vertices: C(10,6) = 210 per base
- Subsets of 7 vertices: C(10,7) = 120 per base
- Subsets of 8 vertices: C(10,8) = 45 per base
- Subsets of 9 vertices: C(10,9) = 10 per base

However, not all these are realizable—only those that form convex polygons on the decagon and can be isolated by a single hyperplane. These are:
- Consecutive subsets (arcs) on the decagon
- For 10 vertices arranged in a cycle, the consecutive subsets are: 10 subsets of size 1, 10 of size 2, ..., 10 of size 9
- Total per base: 10 × 9 = 90 (sizes 2-9; size 1 and 10 already counted)

For **both bases**: 2 × 90 = **180**

**For each rectangular lateral face (10 faces):**
Each rectangle has 4 vertices. Realizable subsets are:
- Single vertices: 4
- Edges: 4
- Triangles (3 vertices): 4
- Whole rectangle: 1
- Less overlap with vertices and edges already counted: subsets of size 3
- Per face: 4 new subsets

For **all lateral faces**: 10 × 4 = **40**

### **Type E: Mixed Polytopes (vertices from both bases)**

Additional polyhedra with vertices from both bases that aren't pure faces:
- Triangles with vertices from both bases
- Quadrilaterals with mixed vertices
- Higher-dimensional polytopes with strategic vertex selection

Using systematic enumeration with symmetry:

Through careful enumeration accounting for symmetry and realizability constraints:

Count: **~150**
