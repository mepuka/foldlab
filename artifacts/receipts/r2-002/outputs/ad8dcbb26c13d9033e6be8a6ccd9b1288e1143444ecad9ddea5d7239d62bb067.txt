# Problem Analysis

## Given Quantities and Conditions

**Given:**
- Three moons: Io, Europa, and Ganymede
- In one Jupiter month, they complete $I$, $E$, and $G$ orbits respectively (positive integers)
- Each moon appears as a full moon at the start of each of its orbits
- The orbits are uniformly spaced throughout the month

**Constraints:**
1. Exactly **54** moments in time with at least one full moon
2. Exactly **11** moments in time with at least two full moons  
3. At least **1** moment in time with all three full moons

## What is Being Asked

**Find:** $I \cdot E \cdot G$ (the product of the three orbit numbers)

## Setup

If we normalize one Jupiter month to the interval $[0, 1)$:
- Io has full moons at times $\frac{0}{I}, \frac{1}{I}, \frac{2}{I}, \ldots, \frac{I-1}{I}$ (total: $I$ times)
- Europa has full moons at times $\frac{0}{E}, \frac{1}{E}, \frac{2}{E}, \ldots, \frac{E-1}{E}$ (total: $E$ times)
- Ganymede has full moons at times $\frac{0}{G}, \frac{1}{G}, \frac{2}{G}, \ldots, \frac{G-1}{G}$ (total: $G$ times)

The constraints translate to counting distinct time points (using inclusion-exclusion):
- $I + E + G - |I \cap E| - |I \cap G| - |E \cap G| + |I \cap E \cap G| = 54$
- $|I \cap E| + |I \cap G| + |E \cap G| - 2|I \cap E \cap G| = 11$
- $|I \cap E \cap G| \geq 1$

where $|I \cap E| = \gcd(I,E)$, etc.
