# Detailed Solution

## Step 1: Determine the common difference constraints

For an arithmetic sequence with $a_1 = 4$ and common difference $d$:
$$a_k = 4 + (k-1)d$$

If 24 and 34 both appear in the sequence:
- For 24: $4 + (m-1)d = 24 \Rightarrow (m-1)d = 20$
- For 34: $4 + (n-1)d = 34 \Rightarrow (n-1)d = 30$

Subtracting these equations:
$$(n-m)d = 10$$

Therefore, $d$ must be a **divisor of 10**.

## Step 2: List all possible integer values of d

The divisors of 10 are:
$$d \in \{-10, -5, -2, -1, 1, 2, 5, 10\}$$

## Step 3: Verify each value of d produces valid sequences

For 24 and 34 to appear at positive integer positions, we need:
- $m = 1 + \frac{20}{d}$ (must be a positive integer)
- $n = 1 + \frac{30}{d}$ (must be a positive integer)

Checking each divisor:

| d | m = 1 + 20/d | n = 1 + 30/d | Valid? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 + 20 = 21 | 1 + 30 = 31 | ✓ |
| -1 | 1 - 20 = -19 | 1 - 30 = -31 | ✗ |
| 2 | 1 + 10 = 11 | 1 + 15 = 16 | ✓ |
| -2 | 1 - 10 = -9 | 1 - 15 = -14 | ✗ |
| 5 | 1 + 4 = 5 | 1 + 6 = 7 | ✓ |
| -5 | 1 - 4 = -3 | 1 - 6 = -5 | ✗ |
| 10 | 1 + 2 = 3 | 1 + 3 = 4 | ✓ |
| -10 | 1 - 2 = -1 | 1 - 3 = -2 | ✗ |

**Valid values:** $d \in \{1, 2, 5, 10\}$

## Step 4: Calculate the 10th term for each valid sequence

Using $a_{10} = a_1 + 9d = 4 + 9d$:

| d | $a_{10} = 4 + 9d$ |
|---|---|
| 1 | $4 + 9(1) = 13$ |
| 2 | $4 + 9(2) = 22$ |
| 5 | $4 + 9(5) = 49$ |
| 10 | $4 + 9(10) = 94$ |

## Step 5: Sum all 10th terms

$$13 + 22 + 49 + 94 = 178$$

**178**
