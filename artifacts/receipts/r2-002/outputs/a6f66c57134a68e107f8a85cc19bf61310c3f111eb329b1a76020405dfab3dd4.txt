# Detailed Verification of Solution

## Step 1: Identify Set S
- $S = \{n : 1 \le n \le 143, \gcd(n, 143) = 1\}$
- $143 = 11 \times 13$
- $|S| = \phi(143) = \phi(11) \times \phi(13) = 10 \times 12 = 120$ ✓

## Step 2: Understand the Condition
We need pairs $(a,b) \in S \times S$ such that $a+b \in S$.

This requires: $\gcd(a+b, 143) = 1$

## Step 3: Apply Chinese Remainder Theorem
Since $143 = 11 \times 13$:
$$\gcd(a+b, 143) = 1 \iff \gcd(a+b, 11) = 1 \text{ AND } \gcd(a+b, 13) = 1$$

Equivalently:
- $a+b \not\equiv 0 \pmod{11}$, AND
- $a+b \not\equiv 0 \pmod{13}$

## Step 4: Count Valid Pairs Modulo 11

Elements coprime to 11 in their range: $\{1, 2, 3, ..., 10\}$ (10 elements)

Total pairs $(a \bmod 11, b \bmod 11)$ with both coprime to 11:
$$10 \times 10 = 100$$

Pairs where $a+b \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$:
- For each $a \in \{1,...,10\}$, exactly one $b$ satisfies $a+b \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$
- Specifically: $b \equiv -a \equiv 11-a \pmod{11}$
- Count: **10 pairs**

**Valid pairs mod 11:** $100 - 10 = \boxed{90}$ ✓

## Step 5: Count Valid Pairs Modulo 13

Elements coprime to 13 in their range: $\{1, 2, 3, ..., 12\}$ (12 elements)

Total pairs $(a \bmod 13, b \bmod 13)$ with both coprime to 13:
$$12 \times 12 = 144$$

Pairs where $a+b \equiv 0 \pmod{13}$:
- For each $a \in \{1,...,12\}$, exactly one $b$ satisfies $a+b \equiv 0 \pmod{13}$
- Specifically: $b \equiv -a \equiv 13-a \pmod{13}$
- Count: **12 pairs**

**Valid pairs mod 13:** $144 - 12 = \boxed{132}$ ✓

## Step 6: Apply CRT to Combine Results

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, each element of $S$ corresponds to a unique pair of residues $(a \bmod 11, a \bmod 13)$.

The total number of valid pairs $(a,b) \in S \times S$ with $a+b \in S$ is:
$$90 \times 132$$

## Step 7: Calculate the Product

$$90 \times 132 = 90 \times (130 + 2)$$
$$= 90 \times 130 + 90 \times 2$$
$$= 11,700 + 180$$
$$= 11,880$$

Verification: $90 \times 132 = 9 \times 10 \times 12 \times 11 = 9 \times 12 \times 110 = 108 \times 110 = 11,880$ ✓

**Final answer (single integer only): 11880**
