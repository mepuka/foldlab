# Solution Plan for Partitioning an 8×8 Square into 3 Rectangles

## Step 1: Identify Partition Structure Types
Determine all topologically distinct ways to arrange 3 rectangles:
- **Type A:** Two rectangles in one row/column, one in another (L-shaped arrangement)
- **Type B:** All three rectangles in a linear strip (horizontal or vertical)
- **Type C:** Other configurations (nested or more complex arrangements)

## Step 2: Enumerate Horizontal Strip Partitions
- For each way to divide the 8×8 square with 2 horizontal lines at heights h₁ and h₂ (where 0 < h₁ < h₂ < 8)
- This creates 3 horizontal strips of heights h₁, (h₂ - h₁), and (8 - h₂)
- Count: C(7,2) = 21 configurations

## Step 3: Enumerate Vertical Strip Partitions
- For each way to divide the 8×8 square with 2 vertical lines at positions v₁ and v₂ (where 0 < v₁ < v₂ < 8)
- This creates 3 vertical strips of widths v₁, (v₂ - v₁), and (8 - v₂)
- Count: C(7,2) = 21 configurations

## Step 4: Enumerate L-Shaped and Two-Row/Column Partitions
- For each valid split point that creates a rectangle and divides remainder into 2 rectangles
- Consider: one vertical split creating a left/right division, then horizontal split on the larger piece
- Consider: one horizontal split creating a top/bottom division, then vertical split on the larger piece
- Systematically count valid configurations for each split point

## Step 5: Enumerate Other Valid Configurations
- Check for configurations where rectangles don't align in simple strips
- Verify each partition completely covers 8×8 with no gaps or overlaps

## Step 6: Sum All Cases
Add counts from Steps 2, 3, 4, and 5 to get the total number of distinct partitions.

## Step 7: Verify Against Constraints
- Confirm each counted partition uses exactly 3 rectangles
- Verify each rectangle has positive integer dimensions
- Ensure no overlaps and complete coverage
