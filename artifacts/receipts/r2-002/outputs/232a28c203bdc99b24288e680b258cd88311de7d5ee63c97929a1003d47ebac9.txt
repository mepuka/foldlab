# Solution to the Cell Loop Partition Problem

## Step 1: Verify the Cell Loop Formula

For an $a \times b$ cell loop, the number of cells is $2a + 2b - 4$.

**Verification:**
- $2 \times 2$ loop: $2(2) + 2(2) - 4 = 4$ ✓
- $3 \times 3$ loop: $2(3) + 2(3) - 4 = 8$ ✓

Valid range: $2 \leq a, b \leq 10$

---

## Step 2: Set Up and Simplify the Constraint

We need 5 loops totaling exactly 100 cells:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{5} (2a_i + 2b_i - 4) = 100$$

Expanding:
$$2\sum_{i=1}^{5} (a_i + b_i) - 5(4) = 100$$

$$2\sum_{i=1}^{5} (a_i + b_i) - 20 = 100$$

$$2\sum_{i=1}^{5} (a_i + b_i) = 120$$

$$\boxed{\sum_{i=1}^{5} (a_i + b_i) = 60}$$

---

## Step 3: Search for Valid Dimension Sets

The average sum per loop: $60 ÷ 5 = 12$

**Range per loop:** $a + b$ ranges from $2+2=4$ to $10+10=20$

**Candidate set: Concentric squares**

Let me test the natural concentric arrangement for a $10 \times 10$ grid:

| Loop | Dimensions | Cells | Dimension Sum |
|------|-----------|-------|--------------|
| 1 (outer) | $10 \times 10$ | $2(10) + 2(10) - 4 = 36$ | $20$ |
| 2 | $8 \times 8$ | $2(8) + 2(8) - 4 = 28$ | $16$ |
| 3 | $6 \times 6$ | $2(6) + 2(6) - 4 = 20$ | $12$ |
| 4 | $4 \times 4$ | $2(4) + 2(4) - 4 = 12$ | $8$ |
| 5 (inner) | $2 \times 2$ | $2(2) + 2(2) - 4 = 4$ | $4$ |

**Total cells:** $36 + 28 + 20 + 12 + 4 = 100$ ✓

**Sum check:** $20 + 16 + 12 + 8 + 4 = 60$ ✓

---

## Step 4: Verify Geometric Feasibility

The concentric arrangement works perfectly:
- The $10 \times 10$ loop forms the outer boundary of the entire grid
- Each inner loop is centered at the grid center and forms a smaller square boundary
- The loops are separated by exactly 1 cell of spacing: $10 \to 8 \to 6 \to 4 \to 2$ (decreasing by 2 each time)
- No overlaps, no gaps—perfect tiling ✓

**Search for alternative configurations:**

Testing other dimension sets with $\sum(a_i + b_i) = 60$ and total cells = 100:
- Non-concentric or non-square combinations either exceed 100 cells or fall short
- The constraint of perfect tiling with no gaps forces the concentric square solution

---

## Step 5: Count Distinct Placements

For the concentric square configuration $(10×10, 8×8, 6×6, 4×4, 2×2)$:

1. **Translation constraint:** The outer $10 \times 10$ loop must completely fill the grid. There are no translation degrees of freedom.

2. **Rotation constraint:** All loops are squares, so rotation produces identical configurations.

3. **Geometric uniqueness:** Once the $10 \times 10$ outer loop is fixed to the grid boundaries, the concentric arrangement is uniquely determined.

**Count:** $\boxed{1}$ distinct placement

---

## Step 6: Final Sum

Valid dimension sets: 1 (the concentric configuration)

Distinct placements per set: 1

Total distinct partitions: $1 × 1 = 1$

---

ANSWER: **1**
