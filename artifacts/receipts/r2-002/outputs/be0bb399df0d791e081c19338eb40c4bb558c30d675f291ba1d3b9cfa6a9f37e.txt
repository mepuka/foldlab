# Detailed Solution

## Step 1: Establish the constraint on $s_4(n)$

For $n < 1000$, I need to find the maximum value of $s_4(n)$ (sum of base-4 digits).

First, convert 999 to base 4:
- $999 ÷ 4 = 249$ remainder $3$
- $249 ÷ 4 = 62$ remainder $1$
- $62 ÷ 4 = 15$ remainder $2$
- $15 ÷ 4 = 3$ remainder $3$
- $3 ÷ 4 = 0$ remainder $3$

So $999 = 33213_4$, giving $s_4(999) = 3 + 3 + 2 + 1 + 3 = 12$.

However, $s_2(n)$ is also bounded: for $n < 1000 < 2^{10}$, we have $s_2(n) \leq 10$ (at most 10 binary digits).

Since we need $s_2(n) = s_4(n)^2$, we have:
$$s_4(n)^2 \leq 10 \implies s_4(n) \leq 3$$

Therefore $s_4(n) \in \{1, 2, 3\}$.

## Step 2: Understand the relationship between $s_2(n)$ and $s_4(n)$

Each base-4 digit can be expressed in binary with its popcount (number of 1-bits):
- $0_4 = 00_2$ → contributes 0
- $1_4 = 01_2$ → contributes 1
- $2_4 = 10_2$ → contributes 1
- $3_4 = 11_2$ → contributes 2

Thus: $s_2(n) = \sum \text{popcount}(\text{base-4 digit})$

## Step 3: Enumerate cases

### **Case 1: $s_4(n) = 1 \Rightarrow s_2(n) = 1$**

Numbers with base-4 digit sum = 1 have exactly one digit equal to 1 and rest are 0:
$$n \in \{1_4, 10_4, 100_4, 1000_4, 10000_4\} = \{1, 4, 16, 64, 256\}$$

Verification:
- $1 = 1_2$: $s_2(1) = 1$, $s_4(1) = 1$ ✓
- $4 = 100_2$: $s_2(4) = 1$, $s_4(4) = 1$ ✓
- $16 = 10000_2$: $s_2(16) = 1$, $s_4(16) = 1$ ✓
- $64 = 1000000_2$: $s_2(64) = 1$, $s_4(64) = 1$ ✓
- $256 = 100000000_2$: $s_2(256) = 1$, $s_4(256) = 1$ ✓

**All 5 numbers work.**

### **Case 2: $s_4(n) = 2 \Rightarrow s_2(n) = 4$**

Base-4 digits summing to 2:
- One digit is 2: contributes popcount(2) = 1. Result: $s_2 = 1 \neq 4$ ✗
- Two digits are 1: each contributes popcount(1) = 1. Result: $s_2 = 2 \neq 4$ ✗

**No solutions in this case.**

### **Case 3: $s_4(n) = 3 \Rightarrow s_2(n) = 9$**

Base-4 digits summing to 3:
- One digit is 3: contributes popcount(3) = 2. Result: $s_2 = 2 \neq 9$ ✗
- One 2 and one 1: contributes $1 + 1 = 2$. Result: $s_2 = 2 \neq 9$ ✗
- Three 1's: contributes $1 + 1 + 1 = 3$. Result: $s_2 = 3 \neq 9$ ✗

**No solutions in this case.**

## Step 4: Calculate the sum

Only solutions are: $\{1, 4, 16, 64, 256\}$

$$\text{Sum} = 1 + 4 + 16 + 64 + 256 = 341$$

**341**
