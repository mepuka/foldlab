# Extended Solution

## Step 1: Rewrite the parabola in standard form

Starting with: $2y = x^2 - 8x + 12$

Divide by 2: $y = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 4x + 6$

Complete the square:
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x + 16 - 16) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 8 + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$$

**Vertex:** $(4, -2)$; **Opens upward** with vertex form

## Step 2: Find the derivative (tangent line slope)

$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2\right] = (x-4)$$

At point $(a, b)$ on the parabola: **tangent slope** $= a - 4$

## Step 3: Apply tangency requirement

For a circle centered at $(4, 39)$ to be tangent to the parabola at $(a, b)$:
- The radius must be **perpendicular** to the tangent line
- **Radius slope:** $\frac{b - 39}{a - 4}$
- **Tangent slope:** $a - 4$

For perpendicularity, the product of slopes equals $-1$:
$$(a - 4) \cdot \frac{b - 39}{a - 4} = -1$$

$$b - 39 = -1$$

$$b = 38$$

## Step 4: Find the tangency points

Substitute $b = 38$ into the parabola equation:
$$38 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2 - 2$$

$$40 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2$$

$$(a-4)^2 = 80$$

$$a - 4 = \pm\sqrt{80} = \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$

$$a = 4 \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$

## Step 5: Calculate the radius for each tangency point

The distance from center $(4, 39)$ to point $(a, 38)$:

$$r = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + (38-39)^2} = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + 1}$$

For both values of $a$:
$$r^2 = (a-4)^2 + 1 = 80 + 1 = 81$$

$$r = 9$$

**For $a = 4 + 4\sqrt{5}$:** $r_1 = 9$

**For $a = 4 - 4\sqrt{5}$:** $r_2 = 9$

## Step 6: Find the sum of the radii

$$r_1 + r_2 = 9 + 9 = 18$$

## Final Answer

**18**
