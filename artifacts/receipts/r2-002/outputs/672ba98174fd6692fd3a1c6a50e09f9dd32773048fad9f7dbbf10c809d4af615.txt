# Solution: Counting Hamiltonian Paths from A to B in a 3×10 Grid

## Step 1: Understand the Constraint Structure

**Grid Setup:**
- 3 rows × 10 columns = 30 cells total
- A is at position (2, 2) - row 2, column 2
- B is at position (2, 9) - row 2, column 9
- Must find all Hamiltonian paths from A to B (visiting each cell exactly once)

**Key Observation:** Both A and B are in the middle row, separated by 7 cells (columns 3-8), with 1 cell to the left of A and 1 cell to the right of B in row 2.

## Step 2: Analyze the Forced Structure

**Critical Topology:**
- Row 1 has 10 cells, Row 3 has 10 cells
- Row 2 has 10 cells, with A at column 2 and B at column 9
- The path must traverse from column 1 to column 10 (and back within rows)

**Bottleneck Analysis:**
- Before reaching A at (2,2): must visit (2,1), then enter A
- After leaving B at (2,9): must visit (2,10)
- Cells (2,3) through (2,8) form a sequence of 6 cells between A and B

## Step 3: Checkerboard Parity Analysis

**Coloring the grid** (using standard checkerboard where (1,1) is black):
- A at (2,2): black cell (row 2 is even, column 2 is even → even sum = black)
- B at (2,9): white cell (row 2 is even, column 9 is odd → odd sum = white)

**Parity Check:**
- A Hamiltonian path has length 29 (connecting 30 cells)
- Must alternate colors: black → white → black → ... → white
- A (black) to B (white) requires odd length path ✓ (29 edges = odd)
- **This is compatible!**

## Step 4: Trace Forced Path Segments

**Entry to A:**
- A is at (2,2). Before A, we must have (2,1)
- (2,1) can be reached from: (1,1), (2,2)—but (2,2) is A itself
- So before A: must come from (1,1) or (3,1)

**Exit from B:**
- B is at (2,9). After B, we must reach cells not yet visited
- After B: can go to (2,10), (1,9), or (3,9)
- We must end at B, so this is just the exit point

**Core Insight:** The path structure is highly constrained. Let me use systematic case analysis.

## Step 5: Systematic Case Analysis

**The path must:**
1. Enter A from either row 1 or row 3 (coming through column 1)
2. Traverse row 2 visiting cells around A and B
3. Use rows 1 and 3 to "weave" around, visiting all 20 cells there
4. Exit from B back into row 2 or the boundary

**Key Structure:**
The path from A at (2,2) to B at (2,9) in row 2 can visit intermediate cells (2,3) through (2,8) in various orders, BUT:
- The cells in rows 1 and 3 must be visited in a specific way that respects connectivity
- A long thin grid severely limits valid paths

**Critical Realization:** In a 3×10 grid with A and B both in the middle row, the Hamiltonian path structure is almost entirely forced:

- Must make "vertical" excursions to rows 1 and 3
- These excursions must interleave in a compatible way
- The narrow width means few valid interleavings exist
