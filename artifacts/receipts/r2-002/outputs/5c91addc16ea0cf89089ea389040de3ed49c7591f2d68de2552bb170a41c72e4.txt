# Detailed Solution

## Step 1: Identify the Set S
The set $S$ consists of all integers in $\{1, 2, ..., 142\}$ that are coprime to 143.

Since $143 = 11 \times 13$:
$$|S| = \phi(143) = \phi(11) \times \phi(13) = 10 \times 12 = 120$$

## Step 2: Clarify the Problem
We need to count pairs $(a,b) \in S \times S$ such that $a+b \in S$ (working modulo 143).

The condition $a+b \in S$ means: $\gcd(a+b \bmod 143, 143) = 1$

## Step 3: Apply Chinese Remainder Theorem
By CRT, $\gcd(a+b, 143) = 1$ if and only if:
- $\gcd(a+b, 11) = 1$ **AND**
- $\gcd(a+b, 13) = 1$

These conditions are independent modulo 11 and modulo 13.

## Step 4: Count Valid Pairs Modulo 11

Elements of $S$ modulo 11 that are coprime to 11: $\{1, 2, 3, ..., 10\}$ (10 elements)

- Total pairs $(a \bmod 11, b \bmod 11)$: $10 \times 10 = 100$
- **Bad pairs** where $a + b \equiv 0 \pmod{11}$:
  - These are: $(1,10), (2,9), (3,8), (4,7), (5,6), (6,5), (7,4), (8,3), (9,2), (10,1)$
  - Count: 10 pairs

Valid pairs modulo 11: $100 - 10 = \boxed{90}$

## Step 5: Count Valid Pairs Modulo 13

Elements of $S$ modulo 13 that are coprime to 13: $\{1, 2, 3, ..., 12\}$ (12 elements)

- Total pairs $(a \bmod 13, b \bmod 13)$: $12 \times 12 = 144$
- **Bad pairs** where $a + b \equiv 0 \pmod{13}$:
  - These are: $(1,12), (2,11), (3,10), (4,9), (5,8), (6,7), (7,6), (8,5), (9,4), (10,3), (11,2), (12,1)$
  - Count: 12 pairs

Valid pairs modulo 13: $144 - 12 = \boxed{132}$

## Step 6: Apply CRT to Combine Results

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, since the conditions modulo 11 and modulo 13 are independent:

$$\text{Total valid pairs} = 90 \times 132$$

## Step 7: Calculate the Product
$$90 \times 132 = 90 \times (130 + 2) = 11,700 + 180 = 11,880$$
