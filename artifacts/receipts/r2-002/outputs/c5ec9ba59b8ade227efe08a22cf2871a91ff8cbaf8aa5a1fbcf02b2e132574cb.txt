# Step-by-Step Solution

## Step 1: Rewrite the Parabola in Standard Form

Starting with: $2y = x^2 - 8x + 12$

$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x + 12)$$

Complete the square:
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x + 16 - 4)$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}((x-4)^2 - 4)$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$$

**Vertex:** $(4, -2)$ with parabola opening upward.

## Step 2: Find the Tangent Line Condition

Taking the derivative:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2(x-4) = x - 4$$

At any point $(a, b)$ on the parabola, the tangent slope is $m = a - 4$.

## Step 3: Apply the Tangency Requirement

For a circle centered at $(4, 39)$ to be tangent to the parabola at point $(a, b)$:
- The radius from center $(4, 39)$ to $(a, b)$ must be perpendicular to the tangent line
- Radius slope: $\frac{b - 39}{a - 4}$
- Perpendicularity condition: $(a-4) \cdot \frac{b-39}{a-4} = -1$

When $a \neq 4$, this simplifies to:
$$b - 39 = -1$$
$$b = 38$$

## Step 4: Find the Points of Tangency

Substitute $b = 38$ into the parabola equation:
$$38 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2 - 2$$
$$40 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2$$
$$(a-4)^2 = 80$$
$$a - 4 = \pm\sqrt{80} = \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$
$$a = 4 \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$

So we have two tangency points:
- Point 1: $(4 + 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$
- Point 2: $(4 - 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$

## Step 5: Calculate the Radii

For each tangency point, the radius is:
$$r = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + (38-39)^2} = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + 1}$$

**For Point 1** where $a - 4 = 4\sqrt{5}$:
$$r_1 = \sqrt{(4\sqrt{5})^2 + 1} = \sqrt{80 + 1} = \sqrt{81} = 9$$

**For Point 2** where $a - 4 = -4\sqrt{5}$:
$$r_2 = \sqrt{(-4\sqrt{5})^2 + 1} = \sqrt{80 + 1} = \sqrt{81} = 9$$

## Step 6: Find the Sum

$$r_1 + r_2 = 9 + 9 = 18$$

**Verification:** Both points satisfy the parabola equation and the perpendicularity condition. The center $(4, 39)$ is equidistant from both symmetric tangency points.

Final answer (single integer only): **18**
