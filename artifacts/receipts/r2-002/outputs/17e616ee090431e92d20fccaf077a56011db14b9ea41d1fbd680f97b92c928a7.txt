# Detailed Solution to the Cell Loop Partition Problem

## Step 1: Verify the Cell Loop Formula

For an $a \times b$ cell loop (perimeter of a rectangle):
- Top and bottom rows: $2b$ cells
- Left and right columns (excluding corners already counted): $2(a-2)$ cells
- Total: $2b + 2(a-2) = 2a + 2b - 4$ ✓

Valid dimensions: $2 \leq a, b \leq 10$

## Step 2: Establish the Constraint Equation

For 5 loops totaling exactly 100 cells:
$$\sum_{i=1}^{5} (2a_i + 2b_i - 4) = 100$$

$$2\sum_{i=1}^{5} (a_i + b_i) - 20 = 100$$

$$\sum_{i=1}^{5} (a_i + b_i) = 60$$

## Step 3: Search for Valid Dimension Sets

For 5 loops to partition a $10 \times 10$ grid with **no gaps and no overlaps**, we need a special geometric structure.

**Testing Concentric Rectangles (Bullseye Pattern):**

Position loops concentrically with 1-cell spacing:

| Loop | Dimensions | Cells | Sum $(a+b)$ |
|------|-----------|-------|------------|
| Outer | $10 \times 10$ | $2(20)-4=36$ | 20 |
| 2nd | $8 \times 8$ | $2(16)-4=28$ | 16 |
| 3rd | $6 \times 6$ | $2(12)-4=20$ | 12 |
| 4th | $4 \times 4$ | $2(8)-4=12$ | 8 |
| Inner | $2 \times 2$ | $2(4)-4=4$ | 4 |
| **Total** | | **100** | **60** ✓ |

**Verification of geometric feasibility:**
- Outermost $10 \times 10$ at position $(0,0)$ to $(9,9)$ fills the grid perimeter
- Interior forms an $8 \times 8$ region with 64 cells (64 = 36 outer margin cells)
- $8 \times 8$ loop uses 28 cells, leaving $8 \times 8$ interior = 36 cells
- This nests perfectly: $8 \times 8 \to 6 \times 6 \to 4 \times 4 \to 2 \times 2$

**Checking for other valid dimension sets:**

Any other partition of 100 cells into 5 loops would either:
- Create gaps (impossible for a partition)
- Require overlapping loops (violates partition definition)
- Fail to satisfy both the sum constraint and geometric feasibility

The concentric structure is **unique** because:
- The outermost loop MUST be $10 \times 10$ to reach all grid boundaries
- Perfect tiling with no gaps forces each inner loop to occupy exactly the "hole" of the previous loop
- This uniquely determines all dimensions

## Step 4: Determine Geometric Feasibility

The concentric configuration is **geometrically feasible** and **unique** within a $10 \times 10$ grid:
- The $10 \times 10$ perimeter fills the boundary
- All inner loops must be centered concentrically
- No alternative arrangements exist that partition the entire grid

## Step 5: Count Distinct Placements

For the unique dimension set $\{(10,10), (8,8), (6,6), (4,4), (2,2)\}$:

**Translation:** The $10 \times 10$ outer loop must align with the grid boundaries. There is exactly **1 position** (filling the entire perimeter).

**Rotation:** Axis-aligned rectangles don't have meaningful rotations that differ in this context.

**Nesting:** The concentric arrangement is uniquely determined once the outer loop is placed.

**Distinct Configurations: 1**

## Step 6: Sum All Valid Configurations

Total number of distinct partitions of the $10 \times 10$ grid into 5 cell loops:

$$\text{Total} = 1$$
