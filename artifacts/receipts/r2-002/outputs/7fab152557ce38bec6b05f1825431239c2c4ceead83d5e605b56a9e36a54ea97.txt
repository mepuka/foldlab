# Solution Plan

1. **Define variables for initial state**
   - Let $g$ = initial volume of gallium in flask B (in liters)
   - Initial mercury in flask A = 1 liter
   - Initial gallium in flask B = $g$ liters

2. **Analyze Operation 1 (Pour A into B until B is full)**
   - Amount poured from A into B = $1 - g$ liters (to fill flask B to capacity)
   - Mercury transferred to B = $1 - g$ liters
   - Remaining in A: $1 - (1-g) = g$ liters of pure mercury

3. **Analyze the mixture in Flask B after Operation 1**
   - Total volume in B = 1 liter
   - Mercury content in B = $1 - g$ liters
   - Gallium content in B = $g$ liters
   - Mercury concentration in B = $\frac{1-g}{1} = 1 - g$

4. **Analyze Operation 2 (Pour B back into A until A is full)**
   - Amount needed to fill A = $1 - g$ liters
   - This mixture is poured from B into A
   - Mercury poured into A = $(1-g) \times (1-g) = (1-g)^2$ liters

5. **Use the constraint: Final mixture in B is 30% mercury**
   - Remaining in B after Operation 2 = $1 - (1-g) = g$ liters
   - Mercury remaining in B = $(1-g) - (1-g)^2 = (1-g)[1-(1-g)] = (1-g) \cdot g$ liters
   - Set up equation: $\frac{(1-g) \cdot g}{g} = 0.30$
   - Simplify: $1 - g = 0.30$, so $g = 0.70$

6. **Calculate final mercury content in Flask A**
   - Mercury from original A remaining: $g = 0.70$ liters
   - Mercury added from Operation 2: $(1-g)^2 = (0.30)^2 = 0.09$ liters
   - Total mercury in A: $0.70 + 0.09 = 0.79$ liters
   - Mercury percentage in A: $\frac{0.79}{1} \times 100 = 79\%$

**Answer: $x = 79$**
