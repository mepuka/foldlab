# Solution Plan for Hexagon Area Problem

1. **Calculate the circumradius of △ABC**
   - Use Heron's formula to find the area of △ABC
   - Apply $R = \frac{abc}{4K}$ where $K$ is the area

2. **Find the circumcenter O**
   - Set up a coordinate system (place $B$ at origin, $C$ on positive $x$-axis)
   - Use the perpendicular bisector method or solve the system of equations for $O$

3. **Determine the rotation angle θ**
   - Since rotation preserves distances, $A'C' = AC = 15$ and $B'C' = BC = 14$
   - Use the constraint $A'C' \perp B'C'$ to set up an equation
   - Apply the law of cosines in the rotated triangle: $A'B'^2 = A'C'^2 + B'C'^2 - 2(A'C')(B'C')\cos(90°)$
   - Solve for $\theta$ (accounting for the constraint about opposite sides of $B'C'$)

4. **Calculate the coordinates of rotated vertices**
   - Apply rotation matrix: $(x', y') = R_\theta(x, y)$ for each vertex about center $O$
   - Obtain positions of $A'$, $B'$, $C'$

5. **Verify the geometric constraint**
   - Check that $A'$ and $B$ lie on opposite sides of line $B'C'$

6. **Calculate the hexagon area**
   - Use the shoelace formula with vertices in order: $A \to A' \to C' \to C \to B \to B'$
   - Alternatively: $\text{Area}_{\text{hexagon}} = \text{Area}_{\triangle ABC} + \text{Area}_{\triangle A'B'C'} + \text{Area of overlapping regions}$

7. **Round to the nearest integer**
