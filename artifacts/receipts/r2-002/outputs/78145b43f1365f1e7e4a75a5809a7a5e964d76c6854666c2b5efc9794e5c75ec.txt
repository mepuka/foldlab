# Extended Analysis: Hamiltonian Paths on 3×10 Grid from A(2,2) to B(2,9)

## Step 1: Constraint Structure Verification
- Grid: 3 rows × 10 columns = 30 cells total
- Start point A: (2, 2) - middle row, column 2
- End point B: (2, 9) - middle row, column 9
- Requirement: Visit all 30 cells exactly once

## Step 2: Checkerboard Coloring Analysis
Using checkerboard coloring where cell (i,j) is colored by parity of i+j:

- A at (2,2): 2+2 = 4 (EVEN) → Color 1
- B at (2,9): 2+9 = 11 (ODD) → Color 2
- Total cells: 30 (even number)

For a 30-cell Hamiltonian path: Must alternate colors exactly 29 times.
- Path structure: Color1 → Color2 → Color1 → ... → Color1 → Color2
- This requires starting and ending at opposite colors ✓
- A and B satisfy this requirement ✓

## Step 3: Forced Structure in 3×n Grid

A 3-row grid severely constrains paths. Key observations:
- Column 1 (cells at rows 1,2,3): All must be visited and form connected segments
- Columns 2-9: Must form a continuous path
- Column 10 (cells at rows 1,2,3): All must be visited before reaching B

The path must snake through the narrow grid with limited flexibility at transitions between columns.

## Step 4: Row Transition Analysis

Since A and B are both in row 2:
- The path cannot simply snake left-to-right in row 2
- Must visit rows 1 and 3 extensively
- Row transitions are forced at specific points to ensure all cells are visited

Critical constraint: Between A(2,2) and B(2,9), the path must:
- Visit cells (2,3), (2,4), (2,5), (2,6), (2,7), (2,8) in row 2
- Incorporate excursions to rows 1 and 3
- Maintain connectivity throughout
