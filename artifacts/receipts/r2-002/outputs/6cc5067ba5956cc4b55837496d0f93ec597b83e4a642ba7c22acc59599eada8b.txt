# Solution: Partitioning an 8×8 Square into 3 Rectangles

## Step 1: Identify Partition Structure Types

For 3 rectangles, there are two topologically distinct patterns:
- **Type I:** Three rectangles in a single direction (strips)
- **Type II:** One cut in one direction, then a perpendicular cut on one piece (L-shaped/staircase)

No other configurations exist that properly tile a rectangle into exactly 3 rectangular pieces.

## Step 2: Horizontal Strip Partitions

Two horizontal cuts at heights h₁ and h₂ where 0 < h₁ < h₂ < 8:
- h₁ can be chosen from {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7}
- h₂ can be chosen from {h₁+1, ..., 7}
- This is equivalent to choosing 2 distinct values from 7 positions
- **Count: C(7,2) = 21 configurations**

## Step 3: Vertical Strip Partitions

Two vertical cuts at positions v₁ and v₂ where 0 < v₁ < v₂ < 8:
- By identical logic: choosing 2 positions from 7 available
- **Count: C(7,2) = 21 configurations**

## Step 4: L-Shaped and Composite Partitions

**Case 4a: Vertical cut first, then horizontal cut**

First vertical cut at position v (1 ≤ v ≤ 7) creates:
- Left rectangle: v × 8
- Right rectangle: (8-v) × 8

Then cut the **left rectangle** horizontally at height h (1 ≤ h ≤ 7):
- Creates rectangles: v×h, v×(8-h), (8-v)×8
- Configurations: 7 choices for v × 7 choices for h = **49 configurations**

Or cut the **right rectangle** horizontally at height h (1 ≤ h ≤ 7):
- Creates rectangles: v×8, (8-v)×h, (8-v)×(8-h)
- Configurations: 7 × 7 = **49 configurations**

Subtotal: 98 configurations

**Case 4b: Horizontal cut first, then vertical cut**

First horizontal cut at position h (1 ≤ h ≤ 7) creates:
- Top rectangle: 8 × h
- Bottom rectangle: 8 × (8-h)

Then cut the **top rectangle** vertically at position v (1 ≤ v ≤ 7):
- Creates rectangles: v×h, (8-v)×h, 8×(8-h)
- Configurations: 7 × 7 = **49 configurations**

Or cut the **bottom rectangle** vertically at position v (1 ≤ v ≤ 7):
- Creates rectangles: 8×h, v×(8-h), (8-v)×(8-h)
- Configurations: 7 × 7 = **49 configurations**

Subtotal: 98 configurations

## Step 5: Verify No Other Valid Configurations

For 3 rectangles tiling a rectangle, the rectangular partition must be achievable through guillotine cuts. Any such partition falls into one of the categories above:
- All three aligned in one direction (Type I)
- Two aligned in one direction, obtained by cutting perpendicular to the third (Type II)

More complex arrangements would require 4+ rectangles.

## Step 6: Sum All Cases

✓ All rectangles have positive integer dimensions
✓ No overlaps (guillotine cutting ensures proper tiling)
✓ Complete coverage of 8×8 square
✓ All 238 partitions are topologically and dimensionally distinct
