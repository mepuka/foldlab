# Extended Solution: Finding Minimum Divisors for Imbalanced M

## Step 1: Establish the Imbalance Requirement

For $M$ to be imbalanced, we need:
- Number of divisors $d < 0.01M$ to exceed $0.99 \cdot \tau(M)$
- Equivalently: number of divisors $d \geq 0.01M$ must be $\leq \lfloor 0.01\tau(M) \rfloor$

Let's denote "large divisors" as those $\geq 0.01M$.

**Critical observation**: We need $\lfloor 0.01\tau(M) \rfloor \geq 1$ for any imbalanced $M$ to exist (since $M$ itself is always a divisor and $M \geq 0.01M$).

This requires: $\tau(M) \geq 100$

## Step 2: Test $\tau(M) = 100$

For $\tau(M) = 100$:
- Large divisor threshold: $\lfloor 0.01 \cdot 100 \rfloor = 1$
- We can have **at most 1 large divisor** (which must be $M$ itself)
- All other 99 divisors must satisfy: $d < 0.01M$

**Constraint**: If $M$ has smallest prime factor $p_{\min}$, then the second-largest divisor is $M/p_{\min}$.

For imbalance: $M/p_{\min} < 0.01M$
This requires: $p_{\min} > 100$

The smallest prime greater than 100 is **101**.

## Step 3: Construct Candidates with $\tau(M) = 100$

**Option A**: $M = 101^{99}$ gives $\tau(M) = 100$
- Divisors are $101^k$ for $k = 0,1,...,99$
- Second-largest: $101^{98} = M/101 < 0.01M$ ✓
- This works but $M$ is astronomically large

**Option B**: $M = 2^{99} \cdot 101$ gives $\tau(M) = 100 \cdot 2 = 200$ (too many)

**Option C**: We need a more compact form. Consider that for $\tau(M) = (a_1+1)(a_2+1)...$

To get $\tau(M) = 100 = 100 \cdot 1 = 50 \cdot 2 = 25 \cdot 4 = 20 \cdot 5 = 10 \cdot 10 = ...$

The most practical form with $\tau(M) = 100$ and small prime factors would be:
- $M = 2^{99}$ (but 2 < 100, fails requirement)
- $M = 2^{49} \cdot 3^{1}$ gives $\tau = 50 \cdot 2 = 100$, but $M/2$ would be $\geq 0.01M$ ✗
- $M = 2^{24} \cdot 3^{3}$ gives $\tau = 25 \cdot 4 = 100$, but small primes cause issues ✗

## Step 4: Verify $\tau(M) = 100$ is Achievable

After systematic checking, any factorization with $\tau(M) = 100$ using standard small primes creates divisors that are too large relative to $0.01M$.

The only reliable construction requires $M = p^{99}$ where $p > 100$, or equivalently we need $\tau(M) \geq 100$.

## Step 5: Check if Smaller Values Work

For $\tau(M) < 100$: The constraint $\lfloor 0.01\tau(M) \rfloor \geq 1$ fails when $\tau(M) < 100$.

Therefore, the minimum possible value is $\tau(M) = 100$.

However, verifying actual constructibility with $\tau(M) = 100$ shows that $M = 101^{99}$ is the minimal working example.

But if the problem asks for the minimum $\tau(M)$ that **allows** an imbalanced number to exist with standard prime factorizations, the answer is:

## Final Answer

**100**
