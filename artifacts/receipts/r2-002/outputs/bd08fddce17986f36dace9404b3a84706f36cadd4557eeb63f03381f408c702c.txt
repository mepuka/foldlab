# Problem Analysis

## Given Quantities and Conditions

**Given:**
- Seven numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7
- These numbers are written on a blackboard in some initial order (permutation)
- Jacob performs adjacent swaps (bubble sort moves)
- The process continues until the numbers are in ascending order: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7

**Conditions:**
- We are considering initial orderings where it is **possible** that the number 4 was involved in at most one swap during the sorting process
- "It is possible that" means there exists at least one sequence of adjacent swaps that sorts the array while swapping the number 4 at most once

## What Is Being Asked

Count the number of initial permutations of {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7} such that there exists a valid sorting sequence (using only adjacent swaps) where the element 4 participates in at most one swap operation.

**In other words:** How many starting positions for 4 and arrangements of the other numbers allow a valid sorting path where 4 is either:
- Never swapped, or
- Swapped exactly once
