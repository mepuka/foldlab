# Short Numbered Plan for Solving

1. **Establish the divisor structure**
   - If the 6th largest divisor is 6, then n has at least 6 divisors
   - The 5 largest divisors are all > 6, and all remaining divisors are ≤ 6

2. **Identify possible divisors of n that are ≤ 6**
   - These are: 1, 2, 3, 6
   - Since 6 is a divisor of n, we know 2 and 3 both divide n

3. **Determine the 5 largest divisors**
   - The largest divisor is always n itself
   - The other 4 largest divisors (besides n and 6) must be > 6
   - These must be of the form n/p where p is a small prime factor, or divisors between 6 and n

4. **Use the constraint that exactly 5 divisors are > 6**
   - List all divisors of n in descending order
   - Ensure positions 1-5 are > 6, and position 6 equals 6

5. **Systematically check small cases**
   - Consider n = 2^a · 3^b · (other primes) where a,b ≥ 1
   - Generate divisors and count how many exceed 6
   - Verify that the 6th largest is exactly 6

6. **Test candidate values**
   - Check multiples and powers of 2, 3, and products with other small primes
   - Confirm divisor count and ordering for each candidate

7. **Sum all valid values of n**
   - Add together all n that satisfy all three conditions
