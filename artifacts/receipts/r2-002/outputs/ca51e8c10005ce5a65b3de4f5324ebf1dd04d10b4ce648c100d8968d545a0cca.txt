# Solution

## Step 1: Rewrite the parabola in standard form

Starting with: $2y = x^2 - 8x + 12$

Divide by 2: $y = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 4x + 6$

Complete the square:
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x + 16 - 16) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}((x-4)^2 - 16) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 8 + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$$

**Vertex: $(4, -2)$** | Opens upward

## Step 2: Find the tangent line condition

Taking the derivative:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = (x-4)$$

At point $(a, b)$ on the parabola, the tangent line slope is $m = a - 4$

## Step 3: Apply the tangency requirement

The circle center is at $(4, 39)$.

For tangency, the radius to $(a,b)$ must be **perpendicular** to the tangent line.

Slope of radius: $m_r = \frac{b - 39}{a - 4}$

Perpendicularity condition:
$$m_r \cdot m_{\text{tangent}} = -1$$
$$\frac{b - 39}{a - 4} \cdot (a - 4) = -1$$
$$b - 39 = -1$$
$$b = 38$$

## Step 4: Find the points of tangency

Substitute $b = 38$ into the parabola equation:
$$38 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2 - 2$$
$$40 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2$$
$$(a-4)^2 = 80$$
$$a = 4 \pm \sqrt{80} = 4 \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$

The two tangency points are: $(4 + 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$ and $(4 - 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$

## Step 5: Calculate the radius for each tangency point

For both points:
$$r = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + (38-39)^2}$$
$$r = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + 1}$$

Since $(a-4)^2 = 80$ for both values of $a$:
$$r = \sqrt{80 + 1} = \sqrt{81} = 9$$

Both radii equal $r_1 = r_2 = 9$ (symmetric points about the axis of symmetry $x = 4$)

## Step 6: Find the sum

$$r_1 + r_2 = 9 + 9 = 18$$

**18**
