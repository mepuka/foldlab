# Extended Solution

## Step 1: Express each term using geometric series

Starting with the original sum:
$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{10^n - 1}$$

For each term, I'll use the algebraic manipulation:
$$\frac{1}{10^n - 1} = \frac{1}{10^n(1 - 10^{-n})} = \frac{10^{-n}}{1 - 10^{-n}}$$

Applying the geometric series formula $\frac{x}{1-x} = x + x^2 + x^3 + \cdots$ with $x = 10^{-n}$:
$$\frac{10^{-n}}{1 - 10^{-n}} = 10^{-n} + 10^{-2n} + 10^{-3n} + 10^{-4n} + \cdots$$

## Step 2: Rearrange the double sum using divisor counting

Substituting into the original sum:
$$S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 10^{-kn}\right) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} 10^{-kn}$$

Now I change the order of summation by grouping terms with the same product $m = kn$:

For a fixed positive integer $m$, the pairs $(k,n)$ with $kn = m$ correspond exactly to the divisors of $m$. If $n$ divides $m$, then $k = m/n$ gives a valid pair. Therefore:

$$S = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} d_m \cdot 10^{-m}$$

where $d_m = \tau(m)$ is the **number of divisors of $m$**.

## Step 3: Compute $10^{100}S$

Multiplying both sides by $10^{100}$:
$$10^{100}S = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} d_m \cdot 10^{100-m}$$

## Step 4: Identify the integer part

For $m \leq 100$: $10^{100-m} \geq 1$ (contributes to integer part)

For $m > 100$: $0 < 10^{100-m} < 1$ (contributes only to fractional part)

Therefore:
$$\lfloor 10^{100}S \rfloor = \sum_{m=1}^{100} d_m \cdot 10^{100-m}$$

## Step 5: Find remainder modulo 1000

The last three digits depend only on terms where $100 - m \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, i.e., $m \in \{98, 99, 100\}$:

$$\lfloor 10^{100}S \rfloor \equiv d_{98} \cdot 10^2 + d_{99} \cdot 10^1 + d_{100} \cdot 10^0 \pmod{1000}$$

**Computing divisor counts:**

- $98 = 2 \times 7^2$: divisors are $\{1, 2, 7, 14, 49, 98\}$ → $d_{98} = 6$
- $99 = 3^2 \times 11$: divisors are $\{1, 3, 9, 11, 33, 99\}$ → $d_{99} = 6$
- $100 = 2^2 \times 5^2$: divisors are $\{1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100\}$ → $d_{100} = 9$

**Final calculation:**
$$\lfloor 10^{100}S \rfloor \equiv 6(100) + 6(10) + 9(1) \pmod{1000}$$
$$\equiv 600 + 60 + 9 \pmod{1000}$$
$$\equiv 669 \pmod{1000}$$

**669**
