# Solution: Step-by-Step

## Step 1: Rewrite the parabola in standard form

Starting with: $2y = x^2 - 8x + 12$

Divide by 2: $y = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 4x + 6$

Complete the square:
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x + 16 - 16) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}((x-4)^2 - 16) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 8 + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$$

**Vertex:** $(4, -2)$; parabola opens upward ✓

## Step 2: Find the tangent line condition

Taking the derivative:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = (x-4)$$

At a point $(a, b)$ on the parabola, the slope of the tangent line is $m = a - 4$ ✓

## Step 3: Apply the tangency requirement

For the circle centered at $(4, 39)$ to be tangent to the parabola at $(a, b)$, the radius from center to point must be perpendicular to the tangent line.

The slope from $(4, 39)$ to $(a, b)$ is $\frac{b - 39}{a - 4}$.

For perpendicularity:
$$\frac{b - 39}{a - 4} \cdot (a - 4) = -1$$

$$(b - 39) = -1$$

$$b = 38$$ ✓

## Step 4: Find the points of tangency

Substitute $b = 38$ into $y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$:

$$38 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2 - 2$$
$$40 = \frac{1}{2}(a-4)^2$$
$$(a-4)^2 = 80$$
$$a - 4 = \pm\sqrt{80} = \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$
$$a = 4 \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$

So: $a_1 = 4 - 4\sqrt{5}$ and $a_2 = 4 + 4\sqrt{5}$ ✓

## Step 5: Calculate the radius for each tangency point

For any point of tangency $(a, 38)$:
$$r = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + (38-39)^2} = \sqrt{(a-4)^2 + 1}$$

Since $(a-4)^2 = 80$ for both points:

$$r_1 = \sqrt{80 + 1} = \sqrt{81} = 9$$

$$r_2 = \sqrt{80 + 1} = \sqrt{81} = 9$$ ✓

## Step 6: Find the sum

$$r_1 + r_2 = 9 + 9 = 18$$

ANSWER: **18**
