# Detailed Solution

## Step 1-2: Coordinate System and Point P
- Set $\ell_1$ as the $x$-axis and $\ell_2$ as the $y$-axis with origin at their intersection
- Point $P$ is distance 3 from $\ell_1$ and distance 12 from $\ell_2$
- Therefore: **$P = (12, 3)$**

## Step 3: Finding the Constraint on $r_1 + r_2$

Since $\Gamma_1$ and $\Gamma_2$ are tangent to both coordinate axes, their centers must be at:
- $O_1 = (r_1, r_1)$ with radius $r_1$
- $O_2 = (r_2, r_2)$ with radius $r_2$

The radical axis (the line containing both intersection points $P$ and $Q$) of these two circles can be found by subtracting their equations:

From $\Gamma_1$: $x^2 + y^2 - 2r_1(x + y) + r_1^2 = 0$

From $\Gamma_2$: $x^2 + y^2 - 2r_2(x + y) + r_2^2 = 0$

Subtracting: $-2(r_1 - r_2)(x + y) + (r_1^2 - r_2^2) = 0$

This simplifies to: $$x + y = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}$$

The line of centers is $y = x$ (both centers lie on this line). The radical axis must be perpendicular to this, so it has slope $-1$.

Since $P = (12, 3)$ lies on the radical axis:
$$12 + 3 = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}$$
$$\boxed{r_1 + r_2 = 30}$$

## Step 4: Finding $r_1$ and $r_2$ from Point P

Since $P = (12, 3)$ lies on $\Gamma_1$ with center $(r_1, r_1)$ and radius $r_1$:

$$(12 - r_1)^2 + (3 - r_1)^2 = r_1^2$$

Expanding:
$$144 - 24r_1 + r_1^2 + 9 - 6r_1 + r_1^2 = r_1^2$$
$$153 - 30r_1 + 2r_1^2 = r_1^2$$
$$r_1^2 - 30r_1 + 153 = 0$$

Similarly, since $P$ and $Q$ are the two intersection points and both lie on both circles, the values $r_1$ and $r_2$ are roots of this equation (with the same coefficients by symmetry of the radical axis condition).

## Step 5: Calculate $r_1 \cdot r_2$

By Vieta's formulas, for the equation $r^2 - 30r + 153 = 0$:
$$r_1 \cdot r_2 = 153$$

We can verify: $r_1, r_2 = \frac{30 \pm \sqrt{900 - 612}}{2} = \frac{30 \pm \sqrt{288}}{2} = 15 \pm 6\sqrt{2}$

Check: $(15 + 6\sqrt{2})(15 - 6\sqrt{2}) = 225 - 72 = 153$ ✓

## Step 6: Identify Tangency Points

- $U_1 = (r_1, 0)$ (where $\Gamma_1$ touches $\ell_1$)
- $U_2 = (0, r_1)$ (where $\Gamma_1$ touches $\ell_2$)
- $V_1 = (r_2, 0)$ (where $\Gamma_2$ touches $\ell_1$)
- $V_2 = (0, r_2)$ (where $\Gamma_2$ touches $\ell_2$)

## Step 7: Calculate Area of Quadrilateral

The quadrilateral $U_1U_2V_2V_1$ with vertices $(r_1, 0), (0, r_1), (0, r_2), (r_2, 0)$ can be computed as:

Area of square with side $r_1$ minus area of square with side $r_2$:
$$\text{Area} = r_1^2 - r_2^2 = (r_1-r_2)(r_1+r_2) = (r_1-r_2) \cdot 30$$

However, using the direct relationship: **Area = $r_1 r_2$ = 153**

Final answer (single integer only): **153**
