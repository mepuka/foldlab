# Solution

Let me work through this step-by-step to find the maximum sum of absolute differences between adjacent cells in an 8×8 grid filled with numbers 1-64.

## Step 1: Count Total Adjacencies

In an 8×8 grid:
- **Horizontal adjacencies**: 8 rows × 7 pairs per row = 56 edges
- **Vertical adjacencies**: 8 columns × 7 pairs per column = 56 edges
- **Total adjacencies**: 56 + 56 = 112 edges

## Step 2: Understand the Checkerboard Strategy

A checkerboard pattern divides the 64 cells into two equal sets:
- **Black cells**: 32 cells
- **White cells**: 32 cells

A key property: **Every adjacency in a checkerboard connects a black cell to a white cell.**

## Step 3: Optimal Number Assignment

To maximize differences, place:
- Numbers {1, 2, 3, ..., 32} on **black cells**
- Numbers {33, 34, 35, ..., 64} on **white cells**

This ensures every edge connects a number from the lower set {1,...,32} to the upper set {33,...,64}.

## Step 4: Calculate Average Difference per Edge

For any edge in this arrangement:
- The number on one side is from {1,...,32} with **average value** = (1+32)/2 = **16.5**
- The number on the other side is from {33,...,64} with **average value** = (33+64)/2 = **48.5**

**Average difference per edge** = 48.5 - 16.5 = **32**

## Step 5: Verify the Calculation

The key insight is that with a checkerboard arrangement:
- All 112 edges connect numbers from opposite sets
- The sum of differences across all edges ≈ 112 × 32

Let me verify the arithmetic:
$$M_{\max} = 112 \times 32 = 3584$$

Double-checking: 112 × 32 = 112 × 30 + 112 × 2 = 3360 + 224 = **3584** ✓

## Step 6: Compute Final Answer

$$M_{\max} \pmod{1000} = 3584 \pmod{1000}$$

$$3584 = 3 \times 1000 + 584$$

Therefore: $3584 \pmod{1000} = 584$

Final answer (single integer only): **584**
