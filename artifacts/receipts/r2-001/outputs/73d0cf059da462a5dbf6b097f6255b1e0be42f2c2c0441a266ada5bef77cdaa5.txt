# Problem Analysis

## Given Quantities and Conditions

1. **Hemisphere**: radius $R = 200$, sits on top of a horizontal circular disk, shares the same center as the disk
2. **Disk**: radius $R = 200$, horizontal, circular
3. **Small sphere**: radius $r = 42$, can be placed on top of the disk at various positions
4. **Constraint**: The small sphere must lie completely inside the hemisphere
5. **Region $\mathcal{T}$**: Set of all points $P$ on the disk where a sphere of radius $42$ can be placed such that it stays completely inside the hemisphere

## What is Being Asked

Find the ratio $\frac{\text{Area of } \mathcal{T}}{\text{Area of disk}}$ and express it as $\frac{p}{q}$ where $\gcd(p,q) = 1$. Then compute $p + q$.

---

## Setup Clarification

Let me establish coordinates with the disk lying in the $xy$-plane ($z = 0$), centered at the origin. The hemisphere is the upper half of a sphere of radius $200$ centered at the origin, described by $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 200^2$ with $z \geq 0$.

When a sphere of radius $42$ is placed "on top of the disk at point $P$," its center is at height $z = 42$ (resting on the disk), and its center is at position $(P_x, P_y, 42)$ where $P = (P_x, P_y)$ is a point on the disk.

The region $\mathcal{T}$ consists of all points $P$ in the disk such that the sphere of radius $42$ centered at $(P_x, P_y, 42)$ lies completely inside the hemisphere.
