# Solution for Finding Sum of All Valid Radii

## Step 1: Rewrite the parabola in standard form

**What I'm calculating:** Converting the parabola equation to vertex form

**Complete calculation:**
Starting with: $2y = x^2 - 8x + 12$

Divide by 2: $y = \frac{1}{2}x^2 - 4x + 6$

Complete the square:
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 8x + 16 - 16) + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 8 + 6$$
$$y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$$

**Verification:** Vertex is at $(4, -2)$, opening upward. ✓

---

## Step 2: Set up the tangency condition

**What I'm calculating:** The requirement for a circle centered at $(4, 39)$ with radius $r$ to be tangent to the parabola

For tangency, the radius must be perpendicular to the parabola's tangent line at the point of tangency.

---

## Step 3: Use the perpendicularity requirement

**What I'm calculating:** Finding the condition on tangency points using perpendicularity

From the parabola $y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = x - 4$$

For a point $(x, y)$ on the parabola:
- Tangent slope: $m_t = x - 4$
- Radius slope (perpendicular): $m_r = -\frac{1}{x-4}$ (when $x \neq 4$)

The radius connects $(4, 39)$ to $(x, y)$, so:
$$\frac{y-39}{x-4} = -\frac{1}{x-4}$$

**Complete calculation:**
$$(x-4) \cdot \frac{y-39}{x-4} = -1$$
$$y - 39 = -1$$
$$y = 38$$

**Verification:** This makes sense—the perpendicularity condition gives us a constant $y$-value. ✓

---

## Step 4: Solve the system of equations

**What I'm calculating:** Finding all $x$-coordinates where $y = 38$ on the parabola

Substitute $y = 38$ into $y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$:
$$38 = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2 - 2$$
$$40 = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)^2$$
$$80 = (x-4)^2$$
$$x - 4 = \pm\sqrt{80} = \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$
$$x = 4 \pm 4\sqrt{5}$$

**Verification:** Two solutions as expected for a parabola and horizontal line. ✓

---

## Step 5: Calculate corresponding radii

**What I'm calculating:** The distance from center $(4, 39)$ to each tangency point

**For** $x_1 = 4 + 4\sqrt{5}$, $y_1 = 38$:
$$r_1 = \sqrt{(4 + 4\sqrt{5} - 4)^2 + (38-39)^2}$$
$$r_1 = \sqrt{(4\sqrt{5})^2 + (-1)^2}$$
$$r_1 = \sqrt{80 + 1}$$
$$r_1 = \sqrt{81} = 9$$

**For** $x_2 = 4 - 4\sqrt{5}$, $y_2 = 38$:
$$r_2 = \sqrt{(4 - 4\sqrt{5} - 4)^2 + (38-39)^2}$$
$$r_2 = \sqrt{(-4\sqrt{5})^2 + (-1)^2}$$
$$r_2 = \sqrt{80 + 1}$$
$$r_2 = \sqrt{81} = 9$$

**Verification:** Both points are equidistant from the center, which makes sense by symmetry. ✓

---

## Step 6: Sum all valid radii

**What I'm calculating:** The total of all positive radii

$$\text{Sum} = r_1 + r_2 = 9 + 9 = 18$$

**Verification by substitution:** 
- Point $(4 + 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$: Distance to $(4, 39)$ is $\sqrt{80+1} = 9$ ✓
- Point $(4 - 4\sqrt{5}, 38)$: Distance to $(4, 39)$ is $\sqrt{80+1} = 9$ ✓
- Both points satisfy the tangency condition (perpendicularity) ✓
- The answer is a reasonable integer ✓

---

**FINAL ANSWER: 18**
