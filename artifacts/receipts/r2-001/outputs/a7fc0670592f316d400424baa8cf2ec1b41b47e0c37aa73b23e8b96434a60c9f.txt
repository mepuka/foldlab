# Solution to the Recurrence Relation Sum Problem

## Problem Statement
Given the recurrence relation: $a_{n+1}(a_n + 1)^2 \equiv a_n \pmod{397}$

With boundary conditions: $a_{2026} = 9$ and $a_0 = 1$

Find: $\sum_{i=0}^{2026} a_i \pmod{397}$

## Step 1: Derive the Telescoping Property

Starting from the recurrence relation:
$$a_{n+1}(a_n + 1)^2 \equiv a_n \pmod{397}$$

Since 397 is prime, all nonzero elements have modular inverses. Taking the reciprocal of both sides:
$$\frac{1}{a_{n+1}} \equiv \frac{(a_n + 1)^2}{a_n} \pmod{397}$$

Expanding the right side:
$$\frac{1}{a_{n+1}} \equiv \frac{a_n^2 + 2a_n + 1}{a_n} = a_n + 2 + \frac{1}{a_n} \pmod{397}$$

Rearranging to isolate a difference:
$$\frac{1}{a_{n+1}} - \frac{1}{a_n} \equiv a_n + 2 \pmod{397}$$

## Step 2: Sum the Telescoping Series

Summing both sides from $n = 0$ to $n = 2025$:
$$\sum_{n=0}^{2025} \left(\frac{1}{a_{n+1}} - \frac{1}{a_n}\right) \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{2025} (a_n + 2) \pmod{397}$$

The left side telescopes:
$$\frac{1}{a_{2026}} - \frac{1}{a_0} \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{2025} a_n + 2(2026) \pmod{397}$$

## Step 3: Apply Boundary Conditions

With $a_0 = 1$ and $a_{2026} = 9$:
$$\frac{1}{9} - 1 \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{2025} a_n + 4052 \pmod{397}$$

## Step 4: Compute Modular Inverse of 9

Using the extended Euclidean algorithm:
$$397 = 44 \cdot 9 + 1$$
$$1 = 397 - 44 \cdot 9$$

Therefore: $9^{-1} \equiv -44 \equiv 353 \pmod{397}$

## Step 5: Solve for the Sum

$$353 - 1 \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{2025} a_n + 4052 \pmod{397}$$

$$352 \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{2025} a_n + 4052 \pmod{397}$$

Computing $4052 \pmod{397}$:
$$4052 = 10 \cdot 397 + 82 = 3970 + 82$$

So $4052 \equiv 82 \pmod{397}$

Therefore:
$$352 \equiv \sum_{n=0}^{2025} a_n + 82 \pmod{397}$$

$$\sum_{n=0}^{2025} a_n \equiv 270 \pmod{397}$$

## Step 6: Include $a_{2026}$ in the Final Sum

$$\sum_{n=0}^{2026} a_n = \sum_{n=0}^{2025} a_n + a_{2026}$$

$$\sum_{n=0}^{2026} a_n \equiv 270 + 9 = 279 \pmod{397}$$

## Verification

- The telescoping property correctly captures the structure of the recurrence
- All modular arithmetic operations are valid (397 is prime)
- The boundary conditions are properly applied
- The calculation is internally consistent

**FINAL ANSWER: 279**
