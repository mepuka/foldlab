For triangle ABC with AB = c, AC = b, and BC = a = c (isosceles):

where $r$ is the inradius.

$$AI = \frac{a}{\cos(B-C)} \cdot \frac{\sin(C)}{2\cos((B-C)/2)}$$

For an isosceles triangle with AB = BC = c and AC = b, let me use:
$$AI = \frac{b}{2\cos(A/2)}$$

The semiperimeter is $s = \frac{2c+b}{2}$

Using the distance formula: $AI = \sqrt{s(s-c)}$ and $IC = \sqrt{s(s-c)}$ isn't quite right.

**Better approach:** For isosceles triangle ABC with AB = BC = c, AC = b:
$$AI = \frac{4R \sin(B/2) \sin(C/2)}{\cos((B-C)/2)}$$

Let me use the direct formula:
$$AI = \sqrt{\frac{bc[(b+c)^2 - a^2]}{4(a+b+c)^2}} \text{ (not optimal)}$$

**Cleaner approach:** 
$$AI^2 = \frac{bc \cdot s(s-a)}{(s-b)(s-c)}$$

where $s = \frac{2c+b}{2}$, and $a = c$.

$$IC^2 = \frac{ab \cdot s(s-b)}{(s-a)(s-c)}$$

With $a = c$:
$$s - a = s - c = \frac{b}{2}$$
$$s - b = \frac{2c-b}{2}$$

$$AI^2 = \frac{bc \cdot \frac{2c+b}{2} \cdot \frac{b}{2}}{\frac{2c-b}{2} \cdot \frac{b}{2}} = \frac{bc(2c+b)}{2c-b}$$

$$IC^2 = \frac{c \cdot b \cdot \frac{2c+b}{2} \cdot \frac{2c-b}{2}}{(\frac{b}{2})^2} = \frac{bc(2c+b)(2c-b)}{b^2} = \frac{c(4c^2-b^2)}{b}$$
