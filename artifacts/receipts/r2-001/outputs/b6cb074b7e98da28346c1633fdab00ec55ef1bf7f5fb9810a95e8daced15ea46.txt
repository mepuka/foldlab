# Solution Plan for Finding the Area of Quadrilateral $U_1U_2V_2V_1$

## 1. **Set up a coordinate system**
   - Place $\ell_1$ as the $x$-axis and $\ell_2$ as a line at angle $\theta$ to it
   - Use the distance conditions $d(P, \ell_1) = 3$ and $d(P, \ell_2) = 12$ to locate point $P$
   - Find the angle $\theta$ between the two tangent lines using geometry of the configuration

## 2. **Locate the centers and radii of both circles**
   - The center $O_1$ of $\Gamma_1$ lies on the angle bisector of $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ (equidistant from both tangent lines)
   - The radius $r_1$ of $\Gamma_1$ is the distance from $O_1$ to either tangent line
   - Similarly, find $O_2$ and $r_2$ for circle $\Gamma_2$
   - Use the constraint that both circles pass through $P$ and $Q$ with $PQ = 10$

## 3. **Determine the tangency points**
   - $U_1$ is the point on $\ell_1$ closest to $O_1$ (perpendicular from center to tangent line)
   - $U_2$ is the point on $\ell_2$ closest to $O_1$
   - $V_1$ is the point on $\ell_1$ closest to $O_2$
   - $V_2$ is the point on $\ell_2$ closest to $O_2$

## 4. **Calculate the side lengths of the quadrilateral**
   - Compute distances: $U_1U_2$, $U_2V_2$, $V_2V_1$, $V_1U_1$
   - Note that opposite sides may be parallel or have special properties

## 5. **Apply the area formula**
   - If the quadrilateral is a trapezoid (with $U_1U_2 \parallel V_1V_2$ or $U_1V_1 \parallel U_2V_2$), use: $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}(b_1 + b_2)h$$
   - Otherwise, use the Shoelace formula with coordinates of all four vertices:
   $$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2}|x_1(y_2-y_4) + x_2(y_3-y_1) + x_3(y_4-y_2) + x_4(y_1-y_3)|$$

## 6. **Verify using the angle between tangents**
   - The area can also be expressed in terms of the radii and the angle between the tangent lines
   - Use: $\text{Area} = (r_1 + r_2) \cdot d(\ell_1, \ell_2) \cdot \sin\theta$ where $d(\ell_1,\ell_2)$ is the perpendicular separation
