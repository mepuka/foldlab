# Solution: Finding All n Where 6 is the 6th Largest Divisor

## Step 1: Set up the divisor relationship

**What we're calculating:** Establishing the structure of divisors around 6.

If 6 is the 6th largest divisor of $n$, then listing divisors in descending order:
$$d_1 = n > d_2 > d_3 > d_4 > d_5 > d_6 = 6 > d_7 > d_8 > \cdots$$

This means exactly 5 divisors are larger than 6.

**Verification:** This structure makes sense—6 occupies the 6th position with exactly 5 divisors above it.

## Step 2: Determine divisors ≤ 6

**What we're calculating:** Which numbers ≤ 6 must divide $n$.

Since 6 is a divisor of $n$:
- We have $6 | n$, which means $2 | n$ and $3 | n$
- Therefore {1, 2, 3, 6} all divide $n$

Could 4 or 5 divide $n$?
- If $4 | n$: divisors ≤ 6 would be {1, 2, 3, 4, 6} (5 divisors), making 6 the 5th largest—contradiction
- If $5 | n$: divisors ≤ 6 would be {1, 2, 3, 5, 6} (5 divisors), making 6 the 5th largest—contradiction
