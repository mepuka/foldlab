# Solution: Finding the Sum of Five Smallest Values of n

## Step 1: Simplify the Probability Equation

Starting with: $\binom{r}{4}\binom{b}{3} = \binom{r}{5}\binom{b}{2}$

Divide both sides by $\binom{r}{4}$ and $\binom{b}{2}$:
$$\frac{\binom{r}{4}}{\binom{r}{5}} = \frac{\binom{b}{2}}{\binom{b}{3}}$$

Calculate each ratio:
$$\frac{\binom{r}{4}}{\binom{r}{5}} = \frac{\frac{r!}{4!(r-4)!}}{\frac{r!}{5!(r-5)!}} = \frac{5!(r-5)!}{4!(r-4)!} = \frac{5}{r-4}$$

$$\frac{\binom{b}{2}}{\binom{b}{3}} = \frac{\frac{b!}{2!(b-2)!}}{\frac{b!}{3!(b-3)!}} = \frac{3!(b-3)!}{2!(b-2)!} = \frac{3}{b-2}$$

Therefore:
$$\frac{5}{r-4} = \frac{3}{b-2}$$

## Step 2: Derive the Constraint Equation

Cross multiply:
$$5(b-2) = 3(r-4)$$
$$5b - 10 = 3r - 12$$
$$5b = 3r - 2$$

**Constraint:** $b = \frac{3r - 2}{5}$

## Step 3: Apply Validity Constraints

For $b$ to be a positive integer, we need:
- $3r - 2 \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$
- $3r \equiv 2 \pmod{5}$

Since $3 \cdot 2 \equiv 6 \equiv 1 \pmod{5}$, multiply both sides by 2:
$$r \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$$

So valid values are $r \in \{4, 9, 14, 19, 24, 29, 34, 39, ...\}$

We require $r \geq 7$ and $b \geq 7$.
