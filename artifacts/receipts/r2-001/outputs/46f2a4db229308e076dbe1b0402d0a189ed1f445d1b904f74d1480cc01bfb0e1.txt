# Plan to Solve the Imbalanced Number Problem

1. **Understand the imbalanced constraint quantitatively**
   - Recognize that $A > 0.99 \cdot \tau(M)$ means at least $\lceil 0.99 \cdot \tau(M) \rceil + 1$ divisors must be less than $0.01M$
   - This is equivalent to having at most $\lfloor 0.01 \cdot \tau(M) \rfloor$ divisors that are $\geq 0.01M$

2. **Establish the lower bound on divisor count**
   - For the imbalanced condition to be satisfiable, we need enough "small" divisors
   - Determine the minimum $\tau(M)$ such that $\lfloor 0.01 \cdot \tau(M) \rfloor \geq 1$ (allowing at least one large divisor)
   - This gives $\tau(M) \geq 100$

3. **Account for the required prime factorization**
   - Since $2000 = 2^4 \cdot 5^3$ divides $M$, write $M = 2^a \cdot 5^b \cdot p_1^{c_1} \cdots p_k^{c_k}$ where $a \geq 4$, $b \geq 3$
   - Then $\tau(M) = (a+1)(b+1)(c_1+1) \cdots (c_k+1)$

4. **Find the minimal factorization achieving $\tau(M) = 100$**
   - Factor 100: $100 = 2^2 \cdot 5^2 = 4 \cdot 25 = 5 \cdot 20 = 10 \cdot 10$, etc.
   - Check which factorization of 100 is compatible with $a \geq 4$ and $b \geq 3$
   - For example: $100 = 5 \cdot 5 \cdot 4$ corresponds to exponents giving $(a+1) = 5$, $(b+1) = 5$, $(c_1+1) = 4$

5. **Verify the imbalanced condition is satisfiable**
   - Show that there exists a choice of $M$ with $\tau(M) = 100$ (or the minimum achievable value) where the divisor distribution actually permits more than 99 divisors to be less than $0.01M$
   - This requires checking that the smallest divisors are sufficiently numerous

6. **Confirm no smaller value works**
   - Verify that $\tau(M) < 100$ cannot satisfy the imbalanced condition given the constraint $2000 \mid M$
   - State the final answer as the minimum $\tau(M)$
