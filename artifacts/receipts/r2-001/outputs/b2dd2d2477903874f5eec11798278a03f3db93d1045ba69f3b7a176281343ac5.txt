# Solution: Triangles Containing the Hexagon Center

## What We're Calculating

We need to find the number of non-degenerate triangles whose vertices are lattice points in a regular hexagon of side length 4 (placed on a triangular lattice) and whose interiors contain the center O of the hexagon.

## Step 1: Establish Coordinate System

Using Cartesian coordinates with the hexagon center at O = (0, 0):
- A regular hexagon of side length 4 has vertices at distance 4 from the center
- Vertices are at positions: (4cos(kπ/3), 4sin(kπ/3)) for k = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5

The hexagon spans approximately:
- x-range: [-4, 4]
- y-range: [-4√3/2, 4√3/2]

## Step 2: Count Lattice Points in S

For a regular hexagon with side length n on a triangular lattice, the number of lattice points is given by:
$$N = 3n^2 - 3n + 1$$

For n = 4:
$$N = 3(16) - 3(4) + 1 = 48 - 12 + 1 = 37 \text{ points}$$

However, accounting for the full interior and boundary of the hexagon more carefully with dense lattice coverage: **approximately 61-91 lattice points** (depending on exact boundary inclusion).

## Step 3: Identify the Center

The center O = (0, 0) is explicitly at the geometric center of the hexagon and lies strictly in the interior.

## Step 4: Barycentric Coordinate Method

For a triangle ABC with vertices being lattice points, the center O is strictly inside if and only if:
$$O = \alpha A + \beta B + \gamma C$$
where $\alpha, \beta, \gamma > 0$ and $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 1$

Equivalently, using signed areas: O is strictly inside triangle ABC if all three signed areas:
- Area(O, B, C)
- Area(A, O, C)  
- Area(A, B, O)

have the same sign and are all nonzero.

## Step 5: Systematic Enumeration

For a hexagon of side 4, we must:
1. Enumerate all $\binom{N}{3}$ possible triangles formed by lattice points
2. Test each triangle using the barycentric coordinate condition
3. Count only those with O strictly in the interior

With N ≈ 60-90 points, we test roughly $\binom{60}{3}$ to $\binom{90}{3}$ triangles, which is computationally intensive but systematic.

The hexagon possesses 6-fold rotational symmetry about O. Therefore:
- The count of triangles must be invariant under 60° rotations
- The count should be divisible by 6 (or reveal the structure of the fundamental domain)
- If we identify triangles in one 60° sector, multiply by 6
