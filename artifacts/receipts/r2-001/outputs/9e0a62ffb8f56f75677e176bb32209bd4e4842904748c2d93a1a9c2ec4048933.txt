## Step 1: Express $10^9$ in Prime Factorization

**What we're calculating:** The prime factorization of $10^9$

**Calculation:**
$$10^9 = (2 \cdot 5)^9 = 2^9 \cdot 5^9$$

**Verification:** Any divisor of $10^9$ has the form $2^a \cdot 5^b$ where $0 \le a, b \le 9$. This gives $(9+1)(9+1) = 100$ total divisors. ✓

## Step 2: Understand the Complementary Pairing

**What we're calculating:** The pairing relationship between divisors

**Calculation:** For divisor $d = 2^a \cdot 5^b$, its complement is:
$$\frac{10^9}{d} = 2^{9-a} \cdot 5^{9-b}$$

The mapping $(a,b) \leftrightarrow (9-a, 9-b)$ partitions all 100 pairs into complementary pairs.

**Check for fixed points:** A divisor $d$ would pair with itself if $d^2 = 10^9$, requiring $d = 10^{4.5}$, which is not an integer.

**Result:** No self-paired divisors exist. Exactly 50 complementary pairs. ✓
