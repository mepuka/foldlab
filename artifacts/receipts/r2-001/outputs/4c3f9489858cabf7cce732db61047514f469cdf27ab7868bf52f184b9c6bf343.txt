# Problem Analysis

## Given Quantities and Conditions

**Structure:**
- A right prism with two congruent regular decagon bases
- Bottom base: vertices $A_1, A_2, \ldots, A_{10}$
- Top base: vertices $B_1, B_2, \ldots, B_{10}$
- Total vertices: $S$ contains 20 vertices

**Constraints on the dividing plane:**
1. The plane does NOT pass through any vertex in $S$
2. The plane partitions all 20 vertices into exactly two non-empty sets
3. One of these two sets is denoted $M$

## What is Being Asked

Compute the **total number of distinct sets $M$** that can arise as one part of such a partition.

**Important clarification:** Since a plane creates a partition into two complementary sets, and we're counting "possible sets $M$," we must determine whether $\{M, S \setminus M\}$ and $\{S \setminus M, M\}$ represent:
- The same partition (unordered), or  
- Different labeled partitions (ordered)

The problem asks for sets $M$ that "can arise," suggesting we count distinct subsets. However, the phrase "one of which is $M$" indicates we're selecting which part to call $M$. The standard interpretation is to count **unordered partitions** and report the count without double-counting complementary pairs—that is, count partitions where we don't distinguish between the two parts.

Alternatively, if we count all possible sets $M$ that could appear as one side of a valid partition (treating the two sides as distinguishable selections), we would count both a set and its complement separately.

The answer should be the **number of valid partition types** created by planes not passing through vertices of the prism.
