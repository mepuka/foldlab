# Analysis of Competition Math Problem

## Given Quantities and Conditions

**Given Quantities:**
- Three moons: Io, Europa, and Ganymede
- In one Jupiter month, they complete $I$, $E$, and $G$ orbits respectively (positive integers)
- Each moon shows as a full moon at the start of each of its orbits

**Constraints on Full Moon Moments:**
1. Exactly 54 moments with at least one full moon
2. Exactly 11 moments with at least two full moons  
3. At least 1 moment with all three full moons

## Interpretation

The full moons occur at specific moments in time during a Jupiter month:
- Io has full moons at $I$ moments (start of each of its $I$ orbits)
- Europa has full moons at $E$ moments (start of each of its $E$ orbits)
- Ganymede has full moons at $G$ moments (start of each of its $G$ orbits)

These moments are distributed on a timeline with the assumption of a uniform distribution model (or equivalently, that full moon times are well-distributed across the month).

## What is Being Asked

**Find:** $I \cdot E \cdot G$ (the product of the number of orbits completed by each of the three moons in one Jupiter month)

---

## Setup for Solution

Using inclusion-exclusion principle, if we denote:
- $n_1$ = moments with exactly one full moon
- $n_2$ = moments with exactly two full moons
- $n_3$ = moments with all three full moons

Then:
- $n_1 + n_2 + n_3 = 54$ (at least one)
- $n_2 + n_3 = 11$ (at least two)
- $n_3 \geq 1$ (all three)

This gives $n_1 = 43$ and $n_2 + n_3 = 11$ with $n_3 \geq 1$.
