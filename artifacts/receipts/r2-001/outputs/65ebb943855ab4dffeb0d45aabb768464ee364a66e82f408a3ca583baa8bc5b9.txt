# Solution

## Step 1: Simplify the Probability Equation

Given: $\binom{r}{4}\binom{b}{3} = \binom{r}{5}\binom{b}{2}$

Expanding the binomial coefficients:
$$\frac{r(r-1)(r-2)(r-3)}{24} \cdot \frac{b(b-1)(b-2)}{6} = \frac{r(r-1)(r-2)(r-3)(r-4)}{120} \cdot \frac{b(b-1)}{2}$$

Dividing both sides by $\frac{r(r-1)(r-2)(r-3)b(b-1)}{1}$ (all non-zero):
$$\frac{b-2}{144} = \frac{r-4}{240}$$

Cross-multiplying:
$$240(b-2) = 144(r-4)$$
$$240b - 480 = 144r - 576$$
$$240b = 144r - 96$$

Dividing by 48:
$$5b = 3r - 2$$

Therefore: $$b = \frac{3r-2}{5}$$

## Step 2: Derive the Modular Constraint

For $b$ to be a positive integer, we need:
$$3r - 2 \equiv 0 \pmod{5}$$
$$3r \equiv 2 \pmod{5}$$

Since $3 \cdot 2 \equiv 1 \pmod{5}$, multiply both sides by 2:
$$r \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$$

So: $r \in \{4, 9, 14, 19, 24, 29, 34, ...\}$

## Step 3: Apply Validity Constraints

We need $r \geq 5$ (for $\binom{r}{5}$ to exist) and $b \geq 3$ (for $\binom{b}{3}$ to exist).

Since $r \equiv 4 \pmod{5}$ and $r \geq 5$, we start with $r = 9$.

## Step 4: Find Valid Pairs and Calculate $n = r + b$

| $r$ | $b = \frac{3r-2}{5}$ | Valid? | $n = r + b$ |
|-----|-------------------|--------|------------|
| 9 | $\frac{25}{5} = 5$ | ✓ | 14 |
| 14 | $\frac{40}{5} = 8$ | ✓ | 22 |
| 19 | $\frac{55}{5} = 11$ | ✓ | 30 |
| 24 | $\frac{70}{5} = 14$ | ✓ | 38 |
| 29 | $\frac{85}{5} = 17$ | ✓ | 46 |
| 34 | $\frac{100}{5} = 20$ | ✓ | 54 |

## Step 5: Verify One Solution

For $r = 9, b = 5$:
$$\binom{9}{4}\binom{5}{3} = 126 \cdot 10 = 1260$$
$$\binom{9}{5}\binom{5}{2} = 126 \cdot 10 = 1260$$ ✓

## Step 6: Sum the Five Smallest Values of $n$

The five smallest values are: $14, 22, 30, 38, 46$

$$14 + 22 + 30 + 38 + 46 = 150$$

**Verification:** 
- $14 + 22 = 36$
- $36 + 30 = 66$
- $66 + 38 = 104$
- $104 + 46 = 150$ ✓

FINAL ANSWER: **150**
