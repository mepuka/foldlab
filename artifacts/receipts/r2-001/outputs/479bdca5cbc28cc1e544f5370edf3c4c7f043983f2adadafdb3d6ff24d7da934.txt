# Complete Solution: Finding the 2026th Smallest Number and Its Divisor Count

## Step 1: Verify the Invariant Property

**What we're calculating:** Confirming that the product of all numbers is preserved under the swap operation.

**Calculation:**
- Property: $a \cdot b = \gcd(a,b) \cdot \text{lcm}(a,b)$ (fundamental property of gcd and lcm)
- When we replace numbers $a$ and $b$ with $\gcd(a,b)$ and $\text{lcm}(a,b)$, the product remains unchanged
- Initial product: $1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdots 2100 = 2100!$

**Verification:** ✓ This is a well-known property, and the product invariant holds throughout all operations.

**What we're calculating:** The unchangeable final state of the board.

**Calculation:**

- Operation on two copies of $k$: $\gcd(k,k) = k$ and $\text{lcm}(k,k) = k$ → no change

**Verification:** ✓ This is the only configuration where no operation can change the board.

## Step 5: Set Up the Divisor Count Formula

**What we're calculating:** $\tau(k)$ where $k = (2100!)^{1/2100}$

**Calculation:**
For $k = (2100!)^{1/2100}$, if $2100! = \prod_p p^{v_p(2100!)}$, then:
$$k = \prod_p p^{v_p(2100!)/2100}$$

The divisor count formula is:
$$\tau(k) = \prod_p \left(\frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100} + 1\right)$$

**What we're calculating:** $\tau(k) = \prod_p (e_p + 1)$ where $e_p = \frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100}$
