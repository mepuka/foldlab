# Step-by-Step Solution: Counting Valid Partitions of a Decagonal Prism

## Step 1: Understand the Geometric Constraint

A decagonal prism has:
- 20 vertices total (10 on top base, 10 on bottom base)
- A plane that doesn't pass through any vertex partitions these vertices into two non-empty sets
- Set $M$ contains vertices on one side; $S \setminus M$ contains vertices on the other side

## Step 2: Analyze How Planes Partition the Vertices

Since the plane doesn't pass through vertices:
- It must intersect edges of the prism
- On the **top decagon**: the plane creates a line that cuts through the decagon, separating vertices into two groups
- On the **bottom decagon**: the plane creates another line that cuts through that decagon, also separating vertices into two groups
- These cuts are **independent** of each other

## Step 3: Count Valid Partitions of a Single Decagon

For a regular decagon with vertices labeled $v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_{10}$ (in order):

A line cutting through the decagon (without passing through vertices) must:
- Intersect exactly 2 edges of the decagon
- Create two groups of vertices separated by these two cut points

**Valid cut positions:**
- We choose 2 distinct edges from the 10 edges of the decagon to cut through
- These 2 cuts separate the 10 vertices into two contiguous arcs
- One arc contains vertices $\{v_i, v_{i+1}, \ldots, v_j\}$ and the other contains the remaining vertices

**Counting the cuts:**
- There are 10 edges in a decagon
- Choose 2 edges to cut: $\binom{10}{2} = 45$ ways
- Each choice creates a valid partition into two non-empty groups

## Step 4: Account for Independence of the Two Bases

The bottom and top decagons can be partitioned **independently**:
- Bottom decagon: 45 possible partitions
- Top decagon: 45 possible partitions
- **Total combinations:** $45 \times 45 = 2,025$

## Step 5: Verify Non-Empty Condition

Each partition created by cutting 2 edges:
- Creates one arc with at least 1 vertex (could be 1 to 9 vertices)
- Creates another arc with at least 1 vertex (could be 1 to 9 vertices)
- Both groups are non-empty ✓

Combined with the top base partition, we ensure both $M$ and $S \setminus M$ are non-empty.

## Step 6: Final Verification

The plane's partition is determined by:
- Which 2 edges are cut in the bottom decagon (45 choices)
- Which 2 edges are cut in the top decagon (45 choices)
- Total: $45 \times 45 = 2,025$ distinct sets $M$
