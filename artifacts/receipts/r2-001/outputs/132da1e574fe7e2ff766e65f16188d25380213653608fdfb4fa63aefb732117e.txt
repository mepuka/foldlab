# Solution: Finding Divisors with Specific Congruences

## Step 1: Express divisors in standard form

First, let me factor $17017$:
$$17017 = 7 \times 2431 = 7 \times 11 \times 221 = 7 \times 11 \times 13 \times 17$$

Therefore:
$$17017^{17} = 7^{17} \times 11^{17} \times 13^{17} \times 17^{17}$$

Any divisor has the form:
$$d = 7^{a} \times 11^{b} \times 13^{c} \times 17^{e} \text{ where } 0 \leq a, b, c, e \leq 17$$

## Step 2: Determine congruence conditions modulo 3

$$7 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}, \quad 11 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}, \quad 13 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}, \quad 17 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$$

Therefore:
$$d \equiv 1^a \times 2^b \times 1^c \times 2^e \equiv 2^{b+e} \pmod{3}$$

For $d \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$:
- Since $2^1 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$ and $2^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$
- We need $2^{b+e} \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$
- **Condition: $b + e$ must be odd** (exactly one of $b, e$ is odd)

## Step 3: Determine congruence conditions modulo 4

$$7 \equiv 3 \pmod{4}, \quad 11 \equiv 3 \pmod{4}, \quad 13 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}, \quad 17 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$$

Therefore:
$$d \equiv 3^a \times 3^b \times 1^c \times 1^e \equiv 3^{a+b} \pmod{4}$$

For $d \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$:
- Since $3^1 \equiv 3 \pmod{4}$ and $3^2 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$
- We need $3^{a+b} \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$
- **Condition: $a + b$ must be even** ($a$ and $b$ have the same parity)

## Step 4: Analyze mod 3 constraints

From $b + e$ is odd:
- Either $b$ is odd and $e$ is even, or
- $b$ is even and $e$ is odd

For exponent choices in range 0-17:
- Odd values: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15, 17 → **9 choices**
- Even values: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16 → **9 choices**

## Step 5: Analyze mod 4 constraints

From $a + b$ is even:
- Either both $a, b$ are odd, or
- Both $a, b$ are even

## Step 6: Combine constraints using independence (gcd(3,4) = 1)

**Case 1: $b$ is odd**
- From mod 4: $a$ must be odd → **9 choices**
- $b$ is odd → **9 choices**
- From mod 3: $e$ must be even → **9 choices**
- Contribution: $9 \times 9 \times 9 = 729$

**Case 2: $b$ is even**
- From mod 4: $a$ must be even → **9 choices**
- $b$ is even → **9 choices**
- From mod 3: $e$ must be odd → **9 choices**
- Contribution: $9 \times 9 \times 9 = 729$

**For exponent $c$:**
- No constraints from either condition
- $c$ can be any value from 0 to 17 → **18 choices**

## Step 7: Calculate total count N

$$N = (729 + 729) \times 18 = 1458 \times 18$$

Computing:
$$1458 \times 18 = 1458 \times (20 - 2) = 29160 - 2916 = 26244$$

**Verification:** $1458 \times 18 = 1458 \times 18 = 26244$ ✓

## Step 8: Find the remainder

$$N \bmod 1000 = 26244 \bmod 1000 = 244$$

FINAL ANSWER: [244]
