# Problem Analysis

## Given Quantities and Conditions

1. **The infinite sum** $S = \frac{1}{9} + \frac{1}{99} + \frac{1}{999} + \frac{1}{9999} + \cdots$

2. **General term structure**: The $n$-th term (for $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$) has the form $\frac{1}{\underbrace{99\cdots9}_{n \text{ nines}}}$

   - Equivalently: $\frac{1}{10^n - 1}$ for the $n$-th term

3. **Implicit condition**: The sum converges (which can be verified since $\frac{1}{10^n - 1} < \frac{1}{10^n}$ and $\sum \frac{1}{10^n}$ converges)

## What is Being Asked

Find the remainder when $\lfloor 10^{100} S \rfloor$ is divided by $1000$.

In other words:
- Compute $10^{100} S$
- Take the greatest integer less than or equal to this value
- Find the remainder when this integer is divided by $1000$ (i.e., find $\lfloor 10^{100} S \rfloor \pmod{1000}$)
