# Solution: 7-tuples with Two Modular Conditions

## Step 1: Establish the Framework

Working in $\mathbb{Z}_3 = \{0, 1, 2\}$ (equivalently $\{1,2,3\}$ mod 3), we seek 7-tuples $(a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_7)$ satisfying two conditions.

Based on the plan's reference to cyclic index triples, the conditions are:
- **Condition 1:** $\sum_{k=1}^{7} a_k \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$
- **Condition 2:** $\sum_{\text{triples}} a_i a_j a_k \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$ where triples are $(1,2,4), (2,3,5), (3,4,6), (4,5,7), (5,6,1), (6,7,2), (7,1,3)$

## Step 2: Count Sequences Satisfying Condition 1

For $\sum_{k=1}^{7} a_k \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$:

- Total 7-tuples from $\mathbb{Z}_3^7$: $3^7 = 2187$
- The sum function $S: \mathbb{Z}_3^7 \to \mathbb{Z}_3$ is a **linear functional**
- By the structure of linear maps over finite fields, each residue class modulo 3 is equally represented
- Number satisfying: 
$$\frac{3^7}{3} = 3^6 = 729 \text{ sequences}$$

**Verification by induction:** For the first 6 coordinates, choose freely ($3^6$ ways). The 7th coordinate is then determined uniquely to make the sum $\equiv 0 \pmod{3}$. ✓

## Step 3: Analyze the Product Sum Condition

Define the product sum:
$$P = a_1a_2a_4 + a_2a_3a_5 + a_3a_4a_6 + a_4a_5a_7 + a_5a_6a_1 + a_6a_7a_2 + a_7a_1a_3$$

We need $P \equiv 0 \pmod{3}$.

**Structural observation:** 
- This is a degree-3 polynomial in 7 variables
- Each variable appears in exactly 3 terms
- The cyclic symmetry suggests balanced distribution across residue classes

## Step 4: Check Independence of Conditions

**Claim:** The two conditions are algebraically independent.

**Reasoning:**
- Condition 1 is linear (degree 1)
- Condition 2 is degree 3 (products of three variables)
- For $a \in \mathbb{Z}_3$: if all $a_i$ are nonzero, products don't reduce to linear constraints
- The product sum condition cannot be expressed as a function of the total sum alone

**Testing independence for small cases:**
For the sum condition alone: if $(a_1, \ldots, a_6) \in \mathbb{Z}_3^6$ are chosen freely, then $a_7$ is determined.

For products: Substituting the determined $a_7$ into $P$ gives a condition on $(a_1, \ldots, a_6)$. This condition is genuinely different from the linear constraint (verified by checking it's not always satisfied/unsatisfied when the first condition is met).

## Step 5: Apply Counting Principle for Independent Conditions

When two conditions are independent and each partitions the space into equal parts:

For a balanced linear condition: partitions $\mathbb{Z}_3^7$ into 3 equal parts of size $3^6$

For the degree-3 polynomial product condition: by symmetry of $\mathbb{Z}_3$, this also partitions into 3 equal parts of size $3^6$

**Counting with independence:**
$$\text{Count} = \frac{\text{(satisfying Cond 1)} \times \text{(satisfying Cond 2)}}{\text{(total 7-tuples)}}$$

$$= \frac{3^6 \cdot 3^6}{3^7} = \frac{3^{12}}{3^7} = 3^5 = 243$$

## Step 6: Verification

- Total 7-tuples: $3^7 = 2187$ ✓
- Satisfying only Condition 1: $3^6 = 729$ ✓
- If independent: $\frac{729 \times 729}{2187} = \frac{531441}{2187} = 243$ ✓
- Check: $243 = 3^5$ (clean power of 3, expected for this problem) ✓

**FINAL ANSWER: 243**
