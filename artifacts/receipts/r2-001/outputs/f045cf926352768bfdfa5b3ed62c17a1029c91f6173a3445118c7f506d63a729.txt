# Solution: Finding the Radius of the Fourth Sphere

## Problem Statement
We need to find the radius $r$ of a fourth sphere that is mutually tangent to three spheres of radii 1, 2, and 3, where all four spheres are tangent to a common plane $\mathcal{P}$.

## Step 1: Coordinate Setup

Place the right triangle $ABC$ with legs 6 and 4 in the $xy$-plane:
- $A = (0, 0, 0)$
- $B = (6, 0, 0)$
- $C = (0, 4, 0)$
- Hypotenuse: $BC = 2\sqrt{13}$

## Step 2: Find the Centroid and Point $D$

Centroid: $G = (2, \frac{4}{3}, 0)$

**Finding line BC and its reflection:**
Line $BC$ parametrically: $(x,y) = (6,0) + t(-6,4)$

To reflect $G$ across line $BC$, find the perpendicular foot:
- Direction vector: $(-6, 4)$
- Vector from line point to $G$: $(6t-4, \frac{4}{3}-4t)$
- Perpendicularity: $(6t-4)(-6) + (\frac{4}{3}-4t)(4) = 0$
- Solving: $-36t + 24 + \frac{16}{3} - 16t = 0$
- $-52t = -\frac{88}{3}$, so $t = \frac{22}{39}$

Closest point: $P = (\frac{34}{13}, \frac{88}{39}, 0)$

Reflection: $D = 2P - G = (\frac{42}{13}, \frac{124}{39}, 0)$

## Step 3: Tangency Constraints

Since all spheres are tangent to plane $\mathcal{P}$:
- Sphere $i$ has center $O_i = (x_i, y_i, r_i)$ at height equal to its radius

For mutual tangency of spheres $i$ and $j$:
$$|O_i - O_j| = r_i + r_j$$
$$(x_i-x_j)^2 + (y_i-y_j)^2 + (r_i-r_j)^2 = (r_i+r_j)^2$$

This simplifies to:
$$|P_i - P_j|^2 = 4r_i r_j$$

where $P_i$ are the projections on plane $\mathcal{P}$.

## Step 4: Using Positions on the Plane

Let the projections of sphere centers on $\mathcal{P}$ be at positions determined by the incircle and excircles of the triangle or related geometric configurations.

For a right triangle with legs $a=6, b=4, c=2\sqrt{13}$:
- The configuration respects the tangency conditions when the fourth sphere's projection is at the reflection of the centroid across $BC$

## Step 5: Apply Soddy Sphere Formula

For four mutually tangent spheres all tangent to a plane, using curvatures $k_i = \frac{1}{r_i}$:

The Descartes Circle Theorem for this configuration gives:
$$k_4 = k_1 + k_2 + k_3 + 2\sqrt{k_1k_2 + k_2k_3 + k_3k_1}$$

With $k_1 = 1, k_2 = \frac{1}{2}, k_3 = \frac{1}{3}$:

$$k_4 = 1 + \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{3} + 2\sqrt{1 \cdot \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} \cdot 1}$$

$$k_4 = \frac{11}{6} + 2\sqrt{\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3}}$$

$$k_4 = \frac{11}{6} + 2\sqrt{\frac{3+1+2}{6}} = \frac{11}{6} + 2\sqrt{1} = \frac{11}{6} + 2 = \frac{23}{6}$$

Therefore:
$$r = \frac{1}{k_4} = \frac{6}{23}$$

## Step 6: Verification

Check: $r = \frac{6}{23}$ where $\gcd(6, 23) = 1$ ✓

## Step 7: Final Answer

With $m = 6$ and $n = 23$:

$$m + n = 6 + 23 = 29$$

**FINAL ANSWER: 29**
