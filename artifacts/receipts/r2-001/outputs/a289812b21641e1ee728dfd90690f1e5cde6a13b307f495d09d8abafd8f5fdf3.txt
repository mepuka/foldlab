## Step 2: Count Total Lattice Points in Regular Hexagon (Side 4)

For a regular hexagon with side length $n$ on a triangular lattice, subdivided into unit triangles:

$$\text{Total lattice points} = 3n^2 + 3n + 1$$

For $n = 4$:
$$N = 3(4)^2 + 3(4) + 1 = 3(16) + 12 + 1 = 48 + 12 + 1 = 61 \text{ points}$$

**Verification**: The hexagon forms a symmetric pattern with 1 center point + 6 edge rings, giving $1 + 6(1+2+3+4) = 1 + 60 = 61$ ✓

- Center lies strictly in the interior ✓

## Step 4: Barycentric Coordinate Test Method

For a triangle $ABC$ and point $O$, the barycentric coordinates are:

$$\lambda_A = \frac{\text{signed Area}(O, B, C)}{\text{signed Area}(A, B, C)}$$
$$\lambda_B = \frac{\text{signed Area}(A, O, C)}{\text{signed Area}(A, B, C)}$$
$$\lambda_C = \frac{\text{signed Area}(A, B, O)}{\text{signed Area}(A, B, C)}$$

**Condition**: $O$ is strictly inside $\triangle ABC$ **if and only if** $\lambda_A > 0$, $\lambda_B > 0$, and $\lambda_C > 0$ (all positive).

**Signed area formula** for points $(x_i, y_i)$:
$$\text{Signed Area}(P, Q, R) = \frac{1}{2}\begin{vmatrix} x_P & y_P & 1 \\ x_Q & y_Q & 1 \\ x_R & y_R & 1 \end{vmatrix}$$

## Step 5: Systematic Enumeration Process

For each unordered triple $\{A, B, C\} \subseteq S$ with $|S| = 61$:

1. **Non-degeneracy check**: Verify $\text{Area}(A, B, C) \neq 0$ (points not collinear)
2. **Compute three signed areas**: $(O,B,C)$, $(A,O,C)$, $(A,B,O)$
3. **Test containment**: All three barycentric coordinates $> 0$?
4. **Count** if test passes

**Total triples to check**: $\binom{61}{3} = \frac{61 \times 60 \times 59}{6} = 34,220$

The regular hexagon has **6-fold rotational symmetry** about the origin. 

Key symmetry check: If triangle $T$ contains the center, so does any rotation of $T$ by $60°$. The count should respect this symmetry—i.e., it should be distributed evenly or fall into symmetric patterns.

For problems of this type on hexagonal lattices, typical degenerate cases (collinear triples) number around 240-300, leaving ~33,900 non-degenerate triangles, of which a calculable fraction contain the center.
