## Step 3: Calculate the Inradius and Insphere Center R

**Volume using coordinates:**
$$V = \frac{1}{6}|(\vec{V_2} - \vec{V_1}) \cdot ((\vec{V_3} - \vec{V_1}) \times (\vec{V_4} - \vec{V_1}))|$$

- $\vec{V_2} - \vec{V_1} = (0, -2, -2)$
- $\vec{V_3} - \vec{V_1} = (-2, 0, -2)$
- $\vec{V_4} - \vec{V_1} = (-2, -2, 0)$

Cross product: $(\vec{V_3} - \vec{V_1}) \times (\vec{V_4} - \vec{V_1})$
$$= \begin{vmatrix} \vec{i} & \vec{j} & \vec{k} \\ -2 & 0 & -2 \\ -2 & -2 & 0 \end{vmatrix} = (-4, 4, 4)$$

Dot product: $(0, -2, -2) \cdot (-4, 4, 4) = 0 - 8 - 8 = -16$

$$V = \frac{1}{6}|-16| = \frac{8}{3}$$

**Surface Area:**

Each face is an equilateral triangle with edge length $2\sqrt{2}$.

Area of one face: $A_{face} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}(2\sqrt{2})^2 = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \cdot 8 = 2\sqrt{3}$

Total surface area: $A = 4 \cdot 2\sqrt{3} = 8\sqrt{3}$

**Inradius:**
$$r = \frac{3V}{A} = \frac{3 \cdot \frac{8}{3}}{8\sqrt{3}} = \frac{8}{8\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$$

**Incenter (by symmetry):**
