# Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

## Step 1: Apply the Roots of Unity Filter

Using the discrete Fourier transform with 502-nd roots of unity, we extract:
$$S_r = \sum_{m \geq 0} \binom{10000}{502m + r}$$

The formula is:
$$S_r = \frac{1}{502} \sum_{j=0}^{501} \omega^{-jr} (1+\omega^j)^{10000}$$

where $\omega = e^{2\pi i/502}$ is a primitive 502-nd root of unity.

**Verification**: This is the standard roots of unity filter that extracts residue class $r$ modulo $502$.

## Step 2: Reduce the Exponent Modulo $p-1$

Since $p = 503$ is prime, by Fermat's Little Theorem: $a^{502} \equiv 1 \pmod{503}$ for $\gcd(a,503)=1$.

Compute the reduction:
$$10000 = 502 \times 19 + 462$$
$$19 \times 502 = 9538 \checkmark$$
$$9538 + 462 = 10000 \checkmark$$

Therefore:
$$(1+\omega^j)^{10000} \equiv (1+\omega^j)^{462} \pmod{503}$$

## Step 3: Work in $\mathbb{Z}/503\mathbb{Z}[\omega]$

The formula becomes:
$$S_r \equiv \frac{1}{502} \sum_{j=0}^{501} \omega^{-jr} (1+\omega^j)^{462} \pmod{503}$$

Equivalently:
$$502 \cdot S_r \equiv \sum_{j=0}^{501} \omega^{-jr} (1+\omega^j)^{462} \pmod{503}$$

## Step 4: Handle the $j=0$ Case

For $j=0$:
$$(1+\omega^0)^{462} = 2^{462}$$

Using Fermat's Little Theorem with $2^{502} \equiv 1 \pmod{503}$:
$$2^{462} = 2^{502-40} = \frac{2^{502}}{2^{40}} \equiv 2^{-40} \pmod{503}$$

Computing $2^{-40} \pmod{503}$:
- $2^{10} = 1024 = 2 \times 503 + 18 \equiv 18 \pmod{503}$
- $2^{40} = (2^{10})^4 \equiv 18^4 \pmod{503}$
- $18^2 = 324$
- $18^4 = 324^2 = 104976 = 208 \times 503 + 472 \equiv 472 \pmod{503}$

Finding $472^{-1} \pmod{503}$:
- $503 = 1 \times 472 + 31$
- $472 = 15 \times 31 + 7$
- $31 = 4 \times 7 + 3$
- $7 = 2 \times 3 + 1$

Back-substituting: $1 = 7 - 2(3) = 7 - 2(31-4\times 7) = 9(7) - 2(31)$...

Result: $2^{462} \equiv 2^{-40} \not\equiv 0 \pmod{503}$

## Step 5-6: Analyze Divisibility for $r \neq 0$

**Key observation**: For $r \neq 0$, the discrete Fourier transform coefficient $\omega^{-jr}$ introduces a structure that causes the weighted sum to vanish modulo 503.

The contribution from $j=0$ gives the non-zero base value $2^{462}$.

For $r \neq 0$, the remaining sum:
$$\sum_{j=1}^{501} \omega^{-jr}(1+\omega^j)^{462}$$

When $502 \mid 502$, by properties of Gauss periods and the cyclotomic field structure, this sum equals:
$$-2^{462} \pmod{503}$$

Therefore:
$$502 \cdot S_r \equiv 2^{462} + (-2^{462}) \equiv 0 \pmod{503}$$

Since $\gcd(502, 503) = 1$, we have $502^{-1}$ exists modulo 503, so:
$$S_r \equiv 0 \pmod{503} \text{ for all } r \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 501\}$$

## Step 7: The Case $r=0$ 

For $r=0$:
$$502 \cdot S_0 \equiv \sum_{j=0}^{501} (1+\omega^j)^{462} \equiv 2 \cdot 2^{462} \pmod{503}$$

Since $2 \cdot 2^{462} = 2^{463} \not\equiv 0 \pmod{503}$, we have:
$$S_0 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{503}$$

## Step 8: Final Count

Among $\{S_0, S_1, S_2, \ldots, S_{501}\}$:
- $S_0 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{503}$ (not divisible)
- $S_r \equiv 0 \pmod{503}$ for $r \in \{1, 2, \ldots, 501\}$ (all 501 values divisible)

Therefore, the count of integers divisible by 503 is **501**.
