# Solution: Step-by-Step Calculation

## Step 1: Set up the time equations

**What we're calculating:** Defining variables for the time each person walks to the park.

- Let $t$ = time (in hours) that Patrick walks until arrival at the park
- Tanya's travel time = $t - 1$ hours (since she starts 1 hour later)
- Jose's travel time = $t - 2$ hours (since he starts 2 hours later)

✓ This makes sense: later starters have less walking time.

---

## Step 2: Write distance equations for each person

**What we're calculating:** Setting up distance = speed × time for each person.

- Patrick's distance: $D = p \cdot t$ (where $p$ is his speed in mph)
- Tanya's distance: $D = (p + 2)(t - 1)$
- Jose's distance: $D = (p + 9)(t - 2)$

✓ Each equation uses their respective speed and time.

---

## Step 3: Set up equality equations

**What we're calculating:** Since all three people travel the same distance to the park, we equate the expressions.

**Equation 1 (Patrick = Tanya):**
$$p \cdot t = (p + 2)(t - 1)$$

**Equation 2 (Patrick = Jose):**
$$p \cdot t = (p + 9)(t - 2)$$

✓ Logical: same destination = same distance traveled.

---

## Step 4: Solve for $p$ using Equation 1

**What we're calculating:** Patrick's walking speed.

$$pt = (p + 2)(t - 1)$$

Expanding the right side:
$$pt = pt - p + 2t - 2$$

Subtracting $pt$ from both sides:
$$0 = -p + 2t - 2$$

Solving for $p$:
$$p = 2t - 2$$

✓ Patrick's speed is expressed in terms of his travel time.

---

## Step 5: Solve for $t$ using Equation 2

**What we're calculating:** Patrick's total travel time in hours.

$$pt = (p + 9)(t - 2)$$

Expanding the right side:
$$pt = pt - 2p + 9t - 18$$

Subtracting $pt$ from both sides:
$$0 = -2p + 9t - 18$$

Rearranging:
$$2p = 9t - 18$$

Substituting $p = 2t - 2$ from Step 4:
$$2(2t - 2) = 9t - 18$$

$$4t - 4 = 9t - 18$$

$$-4 + 18 = 9t - 4t$$

$$14 = 5t$$

$$t = \frac{14}{5} \text{ hours}$$

✓ This is a positive value less than typical walking times, which makes sense.

---

## Step 4 (Revisited): Calculate $p$

**What we're calculating:** Patrick's exact walking speed.

$$p = 2t - 2 = 2 \cdot \frac{14}{5} - 2 = \frac{28}{5} - \frac{10}{5} = \frac{18}{5} \text{ mph}$$

✓ This is approximately 3.6 mph, a reasonable walking speed.

---

## Step 6: Calculate the distance

**What we're calculating:** The distance from school to park.

$$D = p \cdot t = \frac{18}{5} \cdot \frac{14}{5} = \frac{252}{25} \text{ miles}$$

**Verification:** Let me check all three distances match:
- **Patrick:** $D = \frac{18}{5} \times \frac{14}{5} = \frac{252}{25}$ ✓
- **Tanya:** $D = (\frac{18}{5} + 2) \times (\frac{14}{5} - 1) = \frac{28}{5} \times \frac{9}{5} = \frac{252}{25}$ ✓
- **Jose:** $D = (\frac{18}{5} + 9) \times (\frac{14}{5} - 2) = \frac{63}{5} \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{252}{25}$ ✓

---

## Step 7: Express in lowest terms

**What we're calculating:** Ensuring $\frac{m}{n}$ is in lowest terms.

$$D = \frac{252}{25}$$

Finding the GCD:
- $252 = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 7$
- $25 = 5^2$

$$\gcd(252, 25) = 1$$

✓ The fraction is already in lowest terms with $m = 252$ and $n = 25$.

---

## Step 8: Calculate the final answer

**What we're calculating:** $m + n$

$$m + n = 252 + 25 = 277$$

---

## Final Verification

Substituting back: Patrick walks $\frac{18}{5}$ mph for $\frac{14}{5}$ hours to cover $\frac{252}{25}$ miles. Tanya walks $\frac{28}{5}$ mph for $\frac{9}{5}$ hours to cover $\frac{252}{25}$ miles. Jose walks $\frac{63}{5}$ mph for $\frac{4}{5}$ hours to cover $\frac{252}{25}$ miles. ✓ All checks out!

**FINAL ANSWER: 277**
