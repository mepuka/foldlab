## What I'm Calculating

I need to find all arithmetic sequences with first term $a_1 = 4$ that contain both 24 and 34 as terms. Then I'll sum the 10th term ($a_{10}$) across all valid sequences.

## Step 1: Set up the constraint equations

For an arithmetic sequence: $a_n = 4 + (n-1)d$

**For 24 to be a term (at position $m$):**
$$24 = 4 + (m-1)d$$
$$(m-1)d = 20$$

**For 34 to be a term (at position $n$):**
$$34 = 4 + (n-1)d$$
$$(n-1)d = 30$$

## Step 2: Determine divisibility requirements

Since both equations must hold with positive integer positions $m$ and $n$:
- $d$ must divide both 20 and 30
- Find $\gcd(20, 30) = 10$
- Therefore $d \in \{\text{divisors of } 10\}$

## Step 3: Identify the constraint on $d$

Since $a_1 = 4$ and we need both $24 > 4$ and $34 > 4$ as terms:
- For 24: $(m-1)d = 20 > 0$ requires $m - 1 > 0$, so $m \geq 2$
- For 34: $(n-1)d = 30 > 0$ requires $n - 1 > 0$, so $n \geq 2$

This means **$d$ must be positive**.

## Step 4: List and verify valid divisors

The positive divisors of 10 are: $\{1, 2, 5, 10\}$
