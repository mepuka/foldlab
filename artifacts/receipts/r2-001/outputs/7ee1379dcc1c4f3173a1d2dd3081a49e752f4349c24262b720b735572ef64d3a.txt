# Solution: Finding the 2026th Smallest Number and Its Divisor Count

## Step 1: Understand the Invariant

The operation replaces $(a,b)$ with $(\gcd(a,b), \text{lcm}(a,b))$.

**Key property:** $\gcd(a,b) \cdot \text{lcm}(a,b) = a \cdot b$

Therefore, the **product of all numbers remains invariant**:
$$\text{Product} = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdots 2100 = 2100!$$

## Step 2: Identify the Terminal State

The process terminates when no operation changes the board. This occurs when **all 2100 numbers are identical**.

If all numbers equal $k$:
- $\gcd(k,k) = k$ ✓
- $\text{lcm}(k,k) = k$ ✓

The board becomes unchangeable (fixed point reached).

## Step 3: Find the Terminal Value

From the product invariant with all 2100 numbers equal to $k$:
$$k^{2100} = 2100!$$

Therefore:
$$k = (2100!)^{1/2100}$$

## Step 4: Locate the 2026th Smallest Number

In the final configuration, all 2100 numbers equal $k$.

The sorted list is: $k, k, k, \ldots, k$ (2100 copies)

**The 2026th smallest number is simply $k$.**

## Step 5: Calculate the Prime Factorization of $k$

For each prime $p \leq 2100$, use **Legendre's formula** to find $v_p(2100!)$:
$$v_p(2100!) = \left\lfloor \frac{2100}{p} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{2100}{p^2} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{2100}{p^3} \right\rfloor + \cdots$$

The exponent of $p$ in $k$ is:
$$e_p = \frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100}$$

## Step 6: Compute the Exponents

For each prime $p \leq 2100$:

| Prime | $v_p(2100!)$ | $e_p$ |
|-------|--------------|-------|
| 2 | 2097 | 2097/2100 |
| 3 | 1049 | 1049/2100 |
| 5 | 522 | 522/2100 |
| 7 | 348 | 348/2100 |
| ... | ... | ... |

## Step 7: Apply the Divisor Formula

The number of divisors is:
$$\tau(k) = \prod_{p \text{ prime}, p \leq 2100} \left(\frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100} + 1\right)$$

$$= \prod_{p \text{ prime}, p \leq 2100} \left(\frac{v_p(2100!) + 2100}{2100}\right)$$

Since all exponents $e_p = \frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100}$ are **non-integers** (except we need to verify none equal integers), the divisor formula requires:

$$\tau(k) = \prod_{p} \left(\left\lfloor \frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100} \right\rfloor + 1\right)$$

For most primes, $v_p(2100!) < 2100$, so the exponents are fractional, contributing a factor of $(0+1) = 1$ to the divisor count.

Only when $v_p(2100!) \geq 2100$ do we get contributions greater than 1. This occurs only for:
- $p = 2$: $v_2(2100!) = 2097 < 2100$ → contributes 1
- Similar analysis for other small primes shows they contribute 1

Since no prime's exponent in $2100!$ reaches $2 \times 2100$, the divisor formula yields:

$$\boxed{\tau(k) = 1}$$
