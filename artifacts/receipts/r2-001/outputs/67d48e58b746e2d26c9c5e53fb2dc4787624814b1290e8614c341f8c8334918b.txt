## Step 1: Understanding the State Space and Movement System

**What we're calculating**: The fundamental movement mechanics and state representation.

**Movement Rules** (using compass directions):
- Call **Right (R)**: Jacob moves right, Bojac moves down (90° ccw from right)
- Call **Down (D)**: Jacob moves down, Bojac moves right (90° ccw from down)  
- Call **Left (L)**: Jacob moves left, Bojac moves up (90° ccw from left)
- Call **Up (U)**: Jacob moves up, Bojac moves left (90° ccw from up)

Using coordinates (row, col) where both range [0, 7]:

**Key insight**: Movements are **coupled but orthogonal**. When direction D is called:
- Jacob receives displacement D
- Bojac receives displacement CCWROTATE(D)
- Each is independently blocked by board boundaries

**Verification**: This creates meaningful coupling — we cannot move the players completely independently, constraining the reachable set.

## Step 3: Characterizing Movement Independence

**What we're calculating**: The degrees of freedom in the coupled movement system.

**Critical insight**: The four directions {R, D, L, U} create two independent control dimensions:
- **Horizontal axis**: R increases Jacob's col, L decreases it; D increases Bojac's col, U decreases it
- **Vertical axis**: R increases Bojac's row, L decreases it; U increases Jacob's row, D decreases it

The movements are NOT independent (they're coupled through the direction calls), but we CAN reach many states by carefully choosing move sequences.

**Analysis of reachability**:

- The coupling means if Jacob goes right, Bojac must go down
