# Step-by-Step Solution

## Step 1: Set up the divisor relationship

Let the divisors of $n$ in descending order be: $d_1 = n > d_2 > d_3 > d_4 > d_5 > d_6 = 6$

This means 6 must be the 6th largest divisor of $n$.

## Step 2: Identify constraints

Since 6 is the 6th largest divisor:
- Exactly 5 divisors of $n$ are larger than 6: these are $\{n, d_2, d_3, d_4, d_5\}$
- All remaining divisors of $n$ must be $\leq 6$
- The divisors $\leq 6$ form a subset of $\{1, 2, 3, 6\}$

Since 6 divides $n$, we know $1, 2, 3, 6$ all divide $n$.

So $n = 6k$ for some positive integer $k$.

## Step 3: Determine structure

For 6 to be exactly the 6th largest divisor:
- There must be no divisors in the interval $(6, d_5)$ where $d_5$ is the 5th largest divisor
- The next divisor above 6 must be $d_5$ (the 5th largest)
