# Analysis of the Problem

## Given Quantities and Conditions

**Given:**
- The number 143
- $S$ = the set of all positive integers less than 143 that are relatively prime to 143
  - This means: $S = \{n : 1 \leq n < 143, \gcd(n, 143) = 1\}$

**Key Facts (to be derived):**
- $143 = 11 \times 13$ (product of two primes)
- $|S| = \phi(143) = \phi(11)\phi(13) = 10 \times 12 = 120$ (by Euler's totient function)
- Elements of $S$ are exactly those positive integers less than 143 not divisible by 11 or 13

**Conditions on the triple:**
- $a, b, c \in S$ (all three must be in $S$)
- $a + b = c$ (the sum condition)
- The triple must be **ordered**, meaning $(a,b,c)$ and $(b,a,c)$ are counted separately (if $a \neq b$)

## What is Being Asked

**Determine:** The count of ordered triples $(a,b,c)$ where:
- $a \in S$
- $b \in S$  
- $c \in S$
- $a + b = c$

In other words: count all solutions to $a + b = c$ in $S$.
