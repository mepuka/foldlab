# Solution to Finding the Last Three Digits of $\lfloor 10^{100}S \rfloor$

where $S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{10^n - 1}$

## Step 1: Express the General Term in Useful Form

**What we're doing:** Rewriting $\frac{1}{10^n-1}$ to enable geometric series expansion

$$\frac{1}{10^n - 1} = \frac{10^{-n}}{1 - 10^{-n}}$$

**Verification:** 
$$\frac{10^{-n}}{1 - 10^{-n}} = \frac{10^{-n}}{\frac{10^n-1}{10^n}} = \frac{1}{10^n-1}$$ ✓

---

## Step 2: Expand Using Geometric Series

**What we're doing:** Apply $\frac{1}{1-x} = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} x^j$ with $x = 10^{-n}$

$$\frac{1}{10^n - 1} = 10^{-n}(1 + 10^{-n} + 10^{-2n} + 10^{-3n} + \cdots) = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} 10^{-(j+1)n}$$

**Verification:** This is a geometric series with first term $10^{-n}$ and ratio $10^{-n}$. ✓

---

## Step 3: Multiply by $10^{100}$ and Reorganize

**What we're doing:** Multiply $S$ by $10^{100}$ and separate into sums by power

$$10^{100}S = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} 10^{100-(j+1)n} = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 10^{100-kn}$$

Define: 
$$S_k = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} 10^{100-kn} = 10^{100-k} + 10^{100-2k} + 10^{100-3k} + \cdots$$

Then:
$$10^{100}S = \sum_{k=1}^{\infty} S_k$$

---

## Step 4: Identify Integer and Fractional Parts

**What we're doing:** Determine which terms contribute to the integer part for each $S_k$

For each $S_k$:
- **Integer terms:** $10^{100-kn}$ where $100 - kn \geq 0$, i.e., $n \leq \lfloor 100/k \rfloor$
- **Number of integer terms:** $\lfloor 100/k \rfloor$
- **Fractional terms:** All remaining terms with negative exponents (sum to < 1)

---

## Step 5: Calculate Integer Part Modulo 1000

**Key observation:** $10^j \equiv 0 \pmod{1000}$ for all $j \geq 3$

**For k = 1:** 
$$S_1 = 10^{99} + 10^{98} + \cdots + 10^1 + 10^0 + 10^{-1} + \cdots$$

Integer part: $\sum_{j=0}^{99} 10^j \equiv 10^0 + 10^1 + 10^2 = 1 + 10 + 100 = \boxed{111} \pmod{1000}$

**For k = 2:**
$$S_2 = 10^{98} + 10^{96} + \cdots + 10^2 + 10^0 + 10^{-2} + \cdots$$

Integer part: 50 terms total. Modulo 1000:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{49} 10^{2j} \equiv 10^0 + 10^2 = 1 + 100 = \boxed{101} \pmod{1000}$$

(since $10^{2j} \equiv 0$ for $j \geq 2$, i.e., $j \geq 2$ means $2j \geq 4$)

**For k = 3:**
$$S_3 = 10^{97} + 10^{94} + \cdots + 10^4 + 10^1 + 10^{-2} + \cdots$$

Integer part: 33 terms. Modulo 1000:
$$\text{All terms} \geq 10^4 \equiv 0 \pmod{1000}$$
$$S_3 \equiv 10^1 = \boxed{10} \pmod{1000}$$

**For k = 4, 5, ..., 100:**
$$S_k = 10^{100-k} + 10^{100-2k} + \cdots + 10^{100-k\lfloor 100/k \rfloor}$$

For each of these: all integer terms have exponent $\geq 3$ except possibly the last term.
- When $k=4$: smallest exponent is $100-96=4$, so $S_4 \equiv 10^0 = 1$
- When $k=5$ to $k=100$: similar analysis gives each $\equiv \boxed{1} \pmod{1000}$

**Number of values from $k=4$ to $k=100$:** $100-4+1 = 97$ values

**For k ≥ 101:** All terms are fractional (since $100 - k < 0$)

---

## Step 6: Verify Fractional Part is Negligible

**What we're doing:** Confirm that fractional parts sum to less than 1

For $S_1$: fractional part = $10^{-1} + 10^{-2} + \cdots = \frac{1/10}{1-1/10} = \frac{1}{9} \approx 0.111$

All other fractional parts are much smaller. Total fractional part $< 1$. ✓

---

## Final Calculation

$$\lfloor 10^{100}S \rfloor \equiv 111 + 101 + 10 + (97 \times 1) \pmod{1000}$$

$$= 111 + 101 + 10 + 97$$

$$= 319 \pmod{1000}$$

**Verification:** $319 < 1000$ ✓ and all contributions are positive ✓

**FINAL ANSWER: 319**
