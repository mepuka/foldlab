# Solution: Finding the Count of Divisors with Specific Congruence Properties

## Step 1: Express divisors in standard form

First, I'll factor $17017$:
- $17017 = 7 \times 2431$
- $2431 = 11 \times 221$  
- $221 = 13 \times 17$

Therefore: $17017 = 7 \times 11 \times 13 \times 17$

So: $17017^{17} = 7^{17} \times 11^{17} \times 13^{17} \times 17^{17}$

Any divisor has the form: $$d = 7^{a} \times 11^{b} \times 13^{c} \times 17^{e}$$ 
where $0 \leq a, b, c, e \leq 17$ ✓

## Step 2: Determine congruence conditions modulo 3

Finding residues:
- $7 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$
- $11 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$
- $13 \equiv 1 \pmod{3}$
- $17 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$

Therefore: $$d \equiv 1^a \times 2^b \times 1^c \times 2^e \equiv 2^{b+e} \pmod{3}$$

For $d \equiv 2 \pmod{3}$:
Since $2 \equiv -1 \pmod{3}$, we have $2^{b+e} \equiv (-1)^{b+e} \pmod{3}$

**Condition 1:** $b + e$ must be **odd** ✓

## Step 3: Determine congruence conditions modulo 4

Finding residues:
- $7 \equiv 3 \equiv -1 \pmod{4}$
- $11 \equiv 3 \equiv -1 \pmod{4}$
- $13 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$
- $17 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$

Therefore: $$d \equiv (-1)^a \times (-1)^b \times 1^c \times 1^e \equiv (-1)^{a+b} \pmod{4}$$

For $d \equiv 1 \pmod{4}$:

**Condition 2:** $a + b$ must be **even** ✓

## Step 4: Analyze exponent constraints from mod 3 condition

For $b + e$ to be odd, exactly one of $b, e$ must be odd.

Count of odd values in $\{0,1,...,17\}$: 9 (the values $\{1,3,5,...,17\}$)
Count of even values in $\{0,1,...,17\}$: 9 (the values $\{0,2,4,...,16\}$)

Valid $(b,e)$ pairs where $b+e$ is odd: $9 \times 9 + 9 \times 9 = 162$ pairs ✓

## Step 5: Analyze exponent constraints from mod 4 condition

For $a + b$ to be even, both must have the same parity.

Valid $(a,b)$ pairs where $a+b$ is even: $9 \times 9 + 9 \times 9 = 162$ pairs ✓

## Step 6: Apply Combined Constraints

Since both constraints involve $b$, I'll count by fixing $b$ first.

**For each value of $b \in \{0, 1, ..., 17\}$:**

- **Exponent $a$:** Need $a + b$ even
  - If $b$ is even: $a$ must be even → 9 choices
  - If $b$ is odd: $a$ must be odd → 9 choices
  - **Result: 9 choices for $a$ (regardless of $b$'s parity)**

- **Exponent $c$:** Unrestricted
  - **Result: 18 choices for $c$**

- **Exponent $e$:** Need $b + e$ odd
  - If $b$ is even: $e$ must be odd → 9 choices
  - If $b$ is odd: $e$ must be even → 9 choices
  - **Result: 9 choices for $e$ (regardless of $b$'s parity)**

- **Exponent $b$:** Can be any value from 0 to 17
  - **Result: 18 choices for $b$**

## Step 7: Calculate total count N

For each of the 18 values of $b$, there are $9 \times 18 \times 9$ valid 4-tuples:

$$N = 18 \times (9 \times 18 \times 9)$$

Computing step by step:
- $9 \times 18 = 162$
- $162 \times 9 = 1458$
- $18 \times 1458 = 26244$

**Verification:** $18 \times 1458 = 18(1000 + 458) = 18000 + 8244 = 26244$ ✓

## Step 8: Find the remainder

$$N \bmod 1000 = 26244 \bmod 1000$$

$$26244 = 26 \times 1000 + 244$$

$$N \bmod 1000 = 244$$

FINAL ANSWER: **244**
