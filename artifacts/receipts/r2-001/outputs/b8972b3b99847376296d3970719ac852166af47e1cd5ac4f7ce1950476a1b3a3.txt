# Short Plan for Solving the Competition Math Problem

1. **Understand the Invariant**
   - Recognize that each operation preserves the product of all numbers on the board (since $a \cdot b = \gcd(a,b) \cdot \text{lcm}(a,b)$)
   - Calculate the initial product: $\text{Product} = 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdots 2100 = 2100!$

2. **Identify the Terminal State**
   - Determine what configuration makes the board unchangeable under any operation
   - The fixed point occurs when all 2100 numbers are identical (call this value $k$)
   - At this state, swapping any two copies of $k$ yields $\gcd(k,k) = k$ and $\text{lcm}(k,k) = k$

3. **Find the Terminal Value**
   - From the product invariant: $k^{2100} = 2100!$
   - Therefore: $k = (2100!)^{1/2100}$
   - The board terminates with 2100 copies of this single value $k$

4. **Locate the 2026th Smallest Number**
   - In the final configuration with 2100 identical copies of $k$
   - The 2026th smallest number (by position in sorted order) is simply $k$
   - All 2100 numbers equal $k$, so any position gives us $k$

5. **Calculate the Number of Divisors**
   - Find the prime factorization of $k = (2100!)^{1/2100}$
   - For each prime $p \leq 2100$, use Legendre's formula to find $v_p(2100!)$ (the exponent of $p$ in $2100!$)
   - The exponent of $p$ in $k$ is: $\frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100}$
   - Apply the divisor formula: $\tau(k) = \prod_p \left(\frac{v_p(2100!)}{2100} + 1\right)$

6. **Compute and Verify**
   - Calculate the exponents for all primes up to 2100
   - Multiply together all $(e_p + 1)$ terms where $e_p$ is the exponent of prime $p$ in $k$
   - The result is the total number of divisors of the 2026th smallest number
