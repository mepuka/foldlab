# Analysis of the Competition Math Problem

## Given Quantities and Conditions

1. **Pentagon type**: The pentagon is equiangular (all interior angles are equal)
   
2. **Sum of squares of side lengths**: If the five sides have lengths $a, b, c, d, e$, then
   $$a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + d^2 + e^2 = 308$$

3. **Sum of squares of diagonal lengths**: The pentagon has 5 diagonals. If their lengths are $d_1, d_2, d_3, d_4, d_5$, then
   $$d_1^2 + d_2^2 + d_3^2 + d_4^2 + d_5^2 = 800$$

4. **Constraint from equiangular property**: Each interior angle of an equiangular pentagon equals $\frac{(5-2) \cdot 180°}{5} = 108°$

## What is Being Asked

Find the square of the perimeter of the pentagon, expressed in the form $m\sqrt{n}$ where:
- $m$ and $n$ are positive integers
- $n$ is square-free (not divisible by the square of any prime)

Then report $m + n$.

In other words, if the perimeter is $P = a + b + c + d + e$, find $P^2 = m\sqrt{n}$ and compute $m + n$.
