# Solution Plan: Count Permutations of Order Dividing 6

## Step 1: Understand the Constraint
- Since $\pi$ is surjective from a finite set to itself, $\pi$ must be a **permutation** (bijection)
- The condition $\pi^6(a) = a$ for all $a$ means $\pi^6 = \text{identity}$
- This means the **order of $\pi$ divides 6**

## Step 2: Identify Possible Orders
- The divisors of 6 are: **1, 2, 3, and 6**
- Count permutations of each order separately, then sum

## Step 3: Count Permutations of Order 1
- Only the **identity permutation**
- **Count: 1**

## Step 4: Count Permutations of Order 2
- Permutations consisting only of disjoint 2-cycles and fixed points
- Cycle types: $(2)(1)(1)(1)(1)$, $(2)(2)(1)(1)$, $(2)(2)(2)$
- Use the formula: $\frac{n!}{\text{(product of cycle lengths)}^{k_i} \cdot k_i!}$ for each cycle type
- Calculate and sum across valid types
- **Count: 9**

## Step 5: Count Permutations of Order 3
- Permutations consisting only of disjoint 3-cycles and fixed points
- Cycle types: $(3)(1)(1)(1)$, $(3)(3)$
- Apply counting formula for each
- **Count: 8**

## Step 6: Count Permutations of Order 6
- Permutations with at least one 6-cycle, or one 2-cycle and one 3-cycle
- Cycle types: $(6)$, $(3)(2)(1)$
- Apply counting formula for each
- **Count: 144**

## Step 7: Apply Inclusion-Exclusion (if needed)
- Check for overlaps between orders (there are none—an element has a unique order)
- Verify no double-counting

## Step 8: Sum All Counts
$$\text{Total} = 1 + 9 + 8 + 144 = \boxed{162}$$
