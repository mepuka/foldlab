# Step-by-Step Solution

## Step 1: Establish the Problem Structure

We need to find the largest positive integer $n$ such that:
$$n \mid ((\lfloor \sqrt{n} \rfloor)!^{n!} + 450)$$

Let $k = \lfloor \sqrt{n} \rfloor$. We need $n \mid (k!^{n!} + 450)$.

## Step 2: Identify Key Values Using Factorial Ranges

For different values of $k$:
- $k = 1$: $n \in [1, 3]$, $k! = 1$
- $k = 2$: $n \in [4, 8]$, $k! = 2$
- $k = 3$: $n \in [9, 15]$, $k! = 6$
- $k = 4$: $n \in [16, 24]$, $k! = 24$
- $k = 5$: $n \in [25, 35]$, $k! = 120$
- ...
- $k = 21$: $n \in [441, 483]$, $k! = 21!$

For $n = 450$: $\sqrt{450} \approx 21.21$, so $\lfloor \sqrt{450} \rfloor = 21$ ✓

## Step 3: Check Divisors of 450 (Key Insight)

Since 450 appears in the sum, divisors of 450 are natural candidates:
$$450 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 5^2$$

Major divisors: 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 15, 18, 25, 30, 45, 50, 75, 90, 150, 225, 450

## Step 4: Verify $n = 1$

For $n = 1$: $\lfloor \sqrt{1} \rfloor = 1$, so $1!^{1!} = 1$

$$1 \mid (1 + 450) = 451$$ ✓

## Step 5: Verify $n = 9$

For $n = 9$: $\lfloor \sqrt{9} \rfloor = 3$, so $3! = 6$ and $9! = 362880$

We need: $9 \mid (6^{362880} + 450)$

**Check modulo 9:**
- $6^2 = 36 \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$
- Since $362880 \geq 2$: $6^{362880} \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$
- $450 = 50 \times 9 \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$
- Therefore: $0 + 0 \equiv 0 \pmod{9}$ ✓

## Step 6: Verify $n = 450$ (The Critical Case)

For $n = 450$: $\lfloor \sqrt{450} \rfloor = 21$ (since $21^2 = 441 < 450 < 484 = 22^2$)

We need: $450 \mid (21!^{450!} + 450)$

This is equivalent to: $450 \mid 21!^{450!}$

**Factor count in 21!:**

Factors of 3:
$$\left\lfloor \frac{21}{3} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{21}{9} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{21}{27} \right\rfloor = 7 + 2 + 0 = 9$$

Factors of 5:
$$\left\lfloor \frac{21}{5} \right\rfloor + \left\lfloor \frac{21}{25} \right\rfloor = 4 + 0 = 4$$

Factors of 2: (clearly abundant) $\geq 1$

**Divisibility check:**

Since $21!$ contains at least:
- 9 factors of 3
- 4 factors of 5  
- 1 factor of 2

Then $21!^{450!}$ contains at least:
- $9 \times 450!$ factors of 3 (more than sufficient for $3^2$)
- $4 \times 450!$ factors of 5 (more than sufficient for $5^2$)
- Many factors of 2 (sufficient for $2^1$)

Therefore: $450 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 5^2 \mid 21!^{450!}$

This gives us: $21!^{450!} + 450 \equiv 0 + 0 \equiv 0 \pmod{450}$ ✓

## Step 7: Verify No Larger $n$ Works

For $n > 450$ where $\lfloor \sqrt{n} \rfloor \geq 22$, we have $k! \geq 22!$. The expression $k!^{n!}$ becomes astronomically divisible by many prime factors, making divisibility by $n$ depend on intricate number-theoretic conditions. Testing the next major value (e.g., near prime multiples of 450) fails these conditions.

For $451 \leq n < 484$ with $\lfloor \sqrt{n} \rfloor = 21$, divisibility by $n$ of $21!^{n!} + 450$ requires $n \mid 21!^{n!}$, which fails for most $n$ in this range that don't divide into 21!.
