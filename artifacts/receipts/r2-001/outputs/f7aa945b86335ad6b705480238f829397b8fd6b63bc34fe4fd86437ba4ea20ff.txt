Let me work through this systematically, addressing the apparent contradiction and finding the correct interpretation.

## Step 1: Define Variables
- Let $g$ = initial volume of gallium in flask B (liters)
- Initial mercury in flask A = 1 liter
- Let $L$ = capacity of flask B (liters)

## Step 2: Analyze Step 1 (Pour A → B)

When 1 liter of mercury is poured into flask B:
- Flask B receives: 1 liter mercury
- Flask B previously contained: $g$ liters gallium
- Total volume trying to fit in B: $(1 + g)$ liters

**Key constraint:** Flask B has capacity $L$

Since the problem states flask B "fills" and some overflow occurs:
$$1 + g > L$$

The overflow from B consists of:
- When mercury (1 liter) enters the full flask B containing $g$ liters of gallium
- The mixture overflows in proportion to its composition
- Overflow volume = $(1 + g - L)$ liters

**Composition of overflow:**
- Proportion of mercury that entered: $\frac{1}{1+g}$
- Proportion of gallium that was in B: $\frac{g}{1+g}$

Mercury overflowed = $(1 + g - L) \cdot \frac{1}{1+g}$ = $1 - \frac{L}{1+g}$

Mercury remaining in B = $1 - \left(1 - \frac{L}{1+g}\right)$ = $\frac{L}{1+g}$

## Step 3: Use the Final Condition

After Step 2, flask B contains **30% mercury by volume**.

This means:
$$\frac{\text{Mercury in B}}{\text{Total volume in B}} = 0.30$$

Flask B is now full with volume $L$:
$$\frac{L/(1+g)}{L} = 0.30$$

$$\frac{1}{1+g} = 0.30$$

$$1 + g = \frac{1}{0.30} = \frac{10}{3}$$

$$g = \frac{10}{3} - 1 = \frac{7}{3} \text{ liters}$$

## Step 4: Determine Flask B Capacity

From the constraint that B overflows after receiving 1 liter of mercury:
$$1 + g > L$$

But we also know B ends with volume $L$ and concentration 0.30:
$$L = \frac{1}{0.30} \cdot g_{\text{in B}} = \frac{10}{3} \cdot 0.30 \cdot \text{(full capacity)}$$

Since gallium remaining in B after overflow:
$$\text{Gallium in B} = g \cdot \frac{L}{1+g} = \frac{7}{3} \cdot \frac{L}{10/3} = \frac{7L}{10}$$

Mercury in B = $\frac{L}{10/3} = 0.3L$ ✓

For the problem to be consistent: $L < 1 + g = \frac{10}{3}$

A natural choice is $L = 1$ liter (flask B has same capacity as flask A):
- Mercury in B after step 1: $\frac{1}{10/3} = 0.3$ liters
- Gallium in B after step 1: $\frac{7}{10}$ liters
- Total: 1 liter ✓
