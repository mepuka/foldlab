# Visual register — evidence appendix

Status: **EVIDENCE APPENDIX** to
`scratchpad/visual-register-denotation.md`, coordinator-written
2026-08-18 at the operator's direction. Ten inline illustrations, all
self-contained SVG with no external assets, all rendered and read back
this session with `rsvg-convert`.

**What these are.** Citation-images: each shows a tradition's **own**
convention applied to a tiny neutral example, so a reader of the note can
see what a priced candidate actually looks like rather than imagining it.
Where a §3 row's candidates differ visibly, the candidates are drawn side
by side and are **not** labelled by preference.

**What these are not.** None of them is a proposed mark, palette,
typography, or layout for this estate. Every caption says so. Design
verdicts remain out of scope; this file is evidence, and the note it
supports reaches no design conclusions.

**Colour.** Nine of the ten figures define no colour at all and draw in
`currentColor`, so they follow the reader's theme. Figure 4 uses three
hues because demonstrating that hue does **not** carry an order requires
hue; those three values are Bertin's argument, not a palette.

Panel A is composition — boxes joined by a wire. Panel B is the tensor —
boxes with no wire between them. Panel C is the same diagram as B: the
two are equal because the boxes are unconnected, and the coherence
theorem upgrades that from "obviously" to *if and only if*. This is the
picture Selinger uses to introduce the theorem, and it is the note's
spine: **the relative position of two boxes not joined by a wire is not
data.** Note that this holds without symmetry — it is bought by
bifunctoriality of ⊗ alone.

Panel A repeats the unordered case. In panel B a single extra wire (drawn
dashed to distinguish it) is threaded through both boxes, so each takes
it as an input and returns it as an output. Because that wire occurs
exactly once in the diagram, no box can slide past another, and the
interchange law fails. The estate's consequence is in §4.9: **whatever
mark plays this role serializes everything that touches it, whatever it
was drawn to mean** — which is a hazard to name early, since a shared
grid or a global baseline would look like decoration.

The contrast the note's dataflow settlement rests on. In the Kahn drawing
the arc carries a **history**, and moment 1 is a prefix of moment 2 —
cells are added and never removed. In the Petri drawing the arc carries a
**marking**, and firing computes `m − s(τ) + t(τ)`: the input place
strictly loses a token. Both traditions draw nodes with in-arcs and
out-arcs, which is exactly why the difference has to be shown rather than
described. A mark borrowed from the right-hand convention would be false
about a monotone plane.

**technical illustration of feedback monoidal categories's convention — not a proposed style.** Evidences §1.2 and the fold row of §3.2.

A step function with its output looped back through a guard. The
distinction the figure exists to show is that this is **feedback, not
trace**: a trace is feedback plus yanking, and yanking asserts that the
loop is instantaneous. This estate cannot assert that, because the anchor
lags the head — which is why a lag is a drawable quantity at all. The
free such category is the state construction, whose morphisms are exactly
a state space plus a step function, quotiented by change of
representation.

## What was checked, and what was not

**Checked this session.** All ten SVGs parse as XML. All ten were
rasterized with `rsvg-convert` at width 1100 and read back; five were
rebuilt after the first read found overlapping labels, a caption running
off the right edge, and one panel whose bars collided with their own
titles. The five identicon grids in Figure 7 are cell-for-cell the grids
their `sha256` derivations produce. The idempotence claim in Figure 10 is
a hash comparison, quoted above.

**Not checked.** Nothing here was tested on a reader. Legibility at other
sizes, in other themes, or with any colour-vision deficiency is
unexamined — Figure 4's three hues in particular are chosen to be
distinguishable in the author's rendering and are not a safe palette.
Figure 7 makes no claim about how many derived marks a person
distinguishes reliably.

**Standing fences.** Safety only; no illustration here asserts liveness,
and none contains a clock. Every caption states that the convention drawn
is the tradition's own and not a proposal for this estate.
