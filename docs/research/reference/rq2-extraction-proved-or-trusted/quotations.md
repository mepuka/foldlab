## seL4 — Isabelle/HOL, refinement to C, translation validation to binary

Source: <https://sel4.systems/Verification/assumptions.html> ("What the
Proofs Assume"). Site content, licence not stated on the page.

> "Assumptions are not limitations or problems."

> "Being able to clearly state an exhaustive list of assumptions means that
> the work needed to fully trust a system is massively reduced from looking
> at many thousands of lines of code to a number of small, specific, and
> easy to understand pieces."

> "For the proof, we assume this code is correct." — of ~340 lines of
> in-kernel assembly.

> "in this part of the proof, unlike the other parts, the proof is not from
> first principles and there is potential for human error." — of the
> virtual-memory execution model.

> "We assume our prover checks this particular proof correctly."

> "Note that we do not need to trust the compiler and linker any more on
> architectures that are supported by our binary verification."

Source: <https://sel4.systems/About/FAQ.html>

> "The functional correctness proof states that, if the proof assumptions
> are met, the seL4 kernel implementation has no deviations from its
> specification."

> "This means that the compiler does not have to be trusted, and extends
> the functional correctness property to the binary."

Source: Sewell, Myreen, Klein, *Translation Validation for a Verified OS
Kernel*, PLDI 2013, PDF at
<https://trustworthy.systems/publications/nicta_full_text/6449.pdf>

> "We handle binaries generated by unmodified gcc 4.5.1 at optimisation
> level 1, and can handle most of seL4 even at optimisation level 2."

## CompCert — Rocq/Coq, verified compiler, extraction to OCaml

Source: *The CompCert C verified compiler: Documentation and user's manual*,
**Version 3.17**, Xavier Leroy, 13 February 2026,
<https://compcert.org/man/manual001.html>. The manual states it is
distributed under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

> "about 90% of the compiler's algorithms (including all optimizations and
> all code generation algorithms) are proved correct in Rocq/Coq, but the
> remaining 10% (including elaboration, presimplifications, assembling and
> linking) are not verified."

> "only phase 3 (from CompCert C AST to Asm AST) and the parser in phase 2
> are formalized and proved correct in Rocq/Coq."

> "For phase 4 (assembly and linking), we have no formal guarantees yet, but
> the Valex tool, available from AbsInt, provides additional assurance via a
> posteriori validation of the executable produced by the external assembler
> and linker."

The manual does not state a trusted base for the Rocq→OCaml extraction that
produces the compiler binary. Two other projects state it for them; see
EverCrypt and *Verified Extraction* below.

## CakeML — HOL4, proof-producing synthesis plus verified, bootstrapped compiler

Source: Kumar, Myreen, Norrish, Owens, *CakeML: A Verified Implementation of
ML*, POPL 2014, PDF at <https://cakeml.org/popl14.pdf>. ACM copyright.

> "in the context of program verification, an unverified compiler forms a
> large and complex part of the trusted computing base."

> "Lastly, we note that we do not rely on the correctness of another
> compiler in our trusted computing base (except perhaps as part of the
> proof checker or theorem prover)."

> "Having a machine code semantics in the trusted computing base is
> intrinsic to the problem."

Source: <https://cakeml.org/> (front page)

> "It generates CakeML AST from ML-like functions in HOL and proves that the
> generated AST has the same behaviour as the HOL function."

## HACL\* / EverCrypt — F\*, Low\*, KaRaMeL to C

Source: Protzenko et al., *EverCrypt: A Fast, Verified, Cross-Platform
Cryptographic Provider*, IEEE S&P 2020; PDF at
<https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/757.pdf>, §II-C "Trusted Computing Base
(TCB)". (The PDF's text layer renders "F\*" without its star.)

> "As with any verification project, we must trust the correctness of our
> specifications and of our verification tools."

> "To produce executable code, we rely on F\*'s backend for extracting Low\*
> to C code, which can be compiled by using a verified compiler (e.g.,
> CompCert) or by trusting a faster, unverified C compiler."

> "implementations verified in Coq trust Coq, the Coq extraction to OCaml,
> and the OCaml compiler and runtime."

> "This process is trusted, and hence we take steps to enhance its
> trustworthiness." — of porting informal specs into F\*.

Source: KaRaMeL README, <https://github.com/FStarLang/karamel>, Apache-2.0.

> "This work has been formalized on paper. We state that the compilation of
> such F\* programs to C preserves semantics."

## Rocq/Coq extraction — the mechanism itself

Source: *Program extraction*, The Rocq Prover reference manual, version
string on the page **9.4+alpha**,
<https://rocq-prover.org/doc/master/refman/addendum/extraction.html>

> "We present here the Rocq extraction commands, used to build certified and
> relatively efficient functional programs" — the claim is made of the
> programs, not of the extractor.

> "The external function name string is not checked in any way." — of
> `Extract Foreign Constant`.

> "It is the user's responsibility to be sure that no overflow or other bad
> events occur in practice." — of realizing `nat` as OCaml `int`.

Source: Forster, Sozeau, Tabareau, *Verified Extraction from Coq to OCaml*,
PACMPL 8(PLDI), 2024; PDF at <https://hal.science/hal-04329663/document>,
distributed CC BY-NC 4.0.

> "for such executables obtained by extraction, the extraction process is
> part of the trusted code base (TCB), as are Coq's kernel and the compiler
> used to compile the extracted code."

## Isabelle/HOL code generation

Source: Haftmann with Bulwahn and Nipkow, *Code generation from Isabelle/HOL
theories*, dated 18 January 2026 on its title page,
<https://isabelle.in.tum.de/doc/codegen.pdf>

> "every rewrite step performed by the program can be simulated in the
> logic, which guarantees partial correctness"

> "From these steps, only the last two are carried out outside the logic; by
> keeping this layer as thin as possible, the amount of code to trust is
> kept to a minimum."

> "They are safe in the sense that as long as you trust the code generator
> meta theory and implementation, you cannot produce programs that yield
> results which are not derivable in the logic."

> "custom serialisations are completely axiomatic."

On evaluation by oracle — per-run trust, which is not per-run proof:

> "The very presence of the oracle in the code acknowledges that each
> computation requires explicit thinking before it can be considered
> trustworthy!"

## Lean 4 — where its C backend sits

Source: Lean 4.33.0 toolchain sources, Apache-2.0, as installed by elan on
this machine (`leanprover/lean4:v4.33.0`, commit
`d8b18978322de05a8f3dba51ef03cf5461676c17`). Upstream:
<https://github.com/leanprover/lean4>.

`src/lean/Init/Core.lean`, docstring of the axiom `Lean.trustCompiler`:

> "Depends on the correctness of the Lean compiler, interpreter, and all
> `[implemented_by ...]` and `[extern ...]` annotations."

`src/lean/Init/Core.lean`, docstring of `Lean.ofReduceBool`:

> "Warning: by using this feature, the Lean compiler and interpreter become
> part of your trusted code base."

> "Keep in mind that if you are using Lean as programming language, you are
> already trusting the Lean compiler and interpreter."

`src/lean/Lean/Compiler/ImplementedByAttr.lean`, docstring of
`@[implemented_by]`:

> "The provided implementation is not checked to be equivalent to the
> original definition."

> "For a safer variant of this attribute that however doesn't work for
> unsafe implementations, see `@[csimp]`, which requires a proof that the
> two functions are equal."

Source: *The Lean Language Reference*, version string **4.34.0-rc1** at
`/doc/reference/latest/`, chapter 8 (Axioms),
<https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Axioms/>

> "These axioms instead track proofs that depend on the correctness of the
> entire compiler, and not just on the much smaller kernel."

Same manual, §12.4 Foreign Function Interface,
<https://lean-lang.org/doc/reference/latest/Run-Time-Code/Foreign-Function-Interface/>

> "The current interface was designed for internal use in Lean and should be
> considered unstable."

Source: de Moura and Ullrich, *The Lean 4 Theorem Prover and Programming
Language*, CADE 2021; PDF at <https://lean-lang.org/papers/lean4.pdf>

> "Lean has a relatively small trusted kernel"

Its §3 is titled "The Code Generator" and makes no correctness claim about
it.

Source: Lean4Lean README, <https://github.com/digama0/lean4lean>

> "This is an implementation of the Lean 4 kernel written in (mostly) pure
> Lean 4." — the verified re-implementation covers the kernel, not the code
> generator.
